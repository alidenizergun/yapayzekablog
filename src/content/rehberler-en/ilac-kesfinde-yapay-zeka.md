Drug development is a long process that runs from understanding a disease's biology to testing a molecule in humans. AI is being used at different links in this chain. This guide summarizes the AlphaFold story, which led to a Nobel Prize for protein structure prediction, where companies such as Isomorphic Labs stand today, the clinical trial status of AI-discovered drugs, and the realistic limits of the technology. Nothing here is medical advice; consult your doctor for treatment decisions.

## What did AlphaFold do?

Proteins are molecules that perform many jobs in the body, and their functions depend largely on their three-dimensional shapes. Predicting a protein's shape from its amino acid sequence was a problem that went unsolved for decades. According to the Nobel Committee's [press release](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/chemistry/2024/press-release/), Demis Hassabis and John Jumper solved this problem with AlphaFold2, which they unveiled in 2020.

The 2024 Nobel Prize in Chemistry went to Hassabis and Jumper for protein structure prediction, and to David Baker for computational protein design. According to the committee, the prize was worth 11 million Swedish kronor; half went to Baker, and the other half was shared by Hassabis and Jumper.

AlphaFold is based on deep learning methods. This approach uses multilayered artificial neural networks that learn patterns from data. For researchers, the main value lies in being able to see a protein's likely shape without waiting for experimental structure determination.

## The database grows, the limits remain

AlphaFold predictions are stored in the publicly accessible AlphaFold Protein Structure Database. The database is expanding beyond individual proteins to include complexes formed by proteins together. According to a [preprint](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.64898/2026.03.27.714458v1) that has not yet been published in a peer-reviewed journal, the March 2026 update added about 1.8 million high-confidence protein complex predictions. Because preprints have not yet undergone peer review, read this figure as a reported finding rather than a definitive result.

Still, the method has limits. According to a [Phys.org report](https://phys.org/news/2026-06-alphafold-ai-tool-conformation-limitation.html) on a study published in Nature Biotechnology in 2026, AlphaFold3 in some cases completely mispredicts the surface of protein dimers. According to the same report, proteins exist not as a single structure but as moving ensembles. So a predicted shape may not capture the full behavior of a protein in a living cell. This difference matters in drug design, because how a molecule binds to its target protein depends precisely on such details.

## Isomorphic Labs: major funding, no clinical trials yet

Isomorphic Labs, which originated at Google DeepMind, was founded in 2021 according to [information on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isomorphic_Labs), and raised $600 million in its first external funding round in April 2025. The company announced in November 2026 that it had received a $5 billion Series C investment; according to [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/isomorphic-labs-5-billion-series-c-ai-drug-design-2026), Hassabis said more than 20 programs are underway.

The size of the investment does not mean the company has entered the clinical stage. According to [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/173568-isomorphic-labs-raises-5b-to-push-ai-designed-drugs-toward-trials/), the company, which has partnerships with Eli Lilly, Novartis and Johnson & Johnson, has not yet tested any of its candidates in humans. Hassabis said in January 2026 at Davos that he expected the first clinical trials to begin in late 2026 rather than late 2025. According to Dealroom, however, he did not give a current target in his information to Bloomberg; the company said it was 'on track' with its regulatory work and deferred further details until 2027.

According to [BioPharmaTrend's analysis](https://www.biopharmatrend.com/business-intelligence/the-27b-question-how-do-we-know-if-isomorphics-ai-works/), as of September 2026 it could not be verified that the company had filed an investigational new drug (IND) application or announced the start of a trial; the company says its preclinical work is on track. You may see some secondary sites suggesting the company has already passed this stage. Compare such claims with the company's own announcements and regulatory records.

## Drugs found with AI in clinical trials

AI is used not only in protein structure prediction but also in target discovery and molecule design. A frequently cited example is rentosertib, a TNIK inhibitor that Insilico Medicine developed for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). According to a [blog analysis](https://noahrflynn.com/blog/2026/knowledge-graphs-drug-target-discovery-rentosertib/), both the target and the molecule were found using AI, and the drug is described as an 'end-to-end AI-discovered' drug. Note that this source is a blog post rather than a primary paper, so for details you should look at the clinical study itself.

According to the same source, Phase 2a results were published in Nature Medicine in June 2025 and involved 71 patients. This shows that a drug found with AI has been able to reach a peer-reviewed journal with human data. However, a Phase 2a study does not mean the drug has been approved; its effectiveness and safety must be tested in larger and longer studies.

## Realistic expectations and timelines

In drug development, there is a long distance between 'finding the first target' and 'having a drug that reaches patients.' We can summarize this in three points:

- **Structure prediction is not the drug itself.** AlphaFold makes the target easier to understand; the molecule's safety and effectiveness must be demonstrated through experiments.
- **Timelines can slip.** Isomorphic's expectation for its first clinical trial was pushed from late 2025 to late 2026, and the company has not since given a new target. A large investment does not remove this uncertainty.
- **An early clinical signal is not guaranteed success.** As in the rentosertib example, Phase 2a results are a promising step; bringing a drug to market requires broader and longer studies.

So when you read phrases like 'drug discovery is shrinking from years to months,' ask which stage is being discussed. Target discovery or candidate design may speed up; the duration of clinical trials and the approval process are a different matter.

## What to look for when reading the news

- **Check the type of source.** A company announcement, a peer-reviewed paper, a preprint and aggregator sites do not carry the same weight. Many figures spread through secondary sources.
- **Read the stage correctly.** Preclinical work, an IND application, Phase 1, Phase 2 and approval are very different thresholds.
- **Question the scope of 'discovered with AI.'** Was only the target found, was the molecule designed, or both?
- **Compare the company's claim with an independent source.** For unverified information, expecting phrases such as 'reportedly' or 'allegedly' is a healthy habit.
