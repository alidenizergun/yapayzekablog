Collecting notes after a meeting, clarifying decisions and assigning tasks often takes as much time as the meeting itself. AI tools can transcribe recordings, summarize long conversations and list who will do what by when.

## 1. Get consent for the recording

At the start of the meeting, tell participants that it will be recorded and summarized with AI. Some people or topics (performance reviews, sensitive client conversations) may not be suitable for recording. Follow your company's rules on this.

## 2. Transcribe the recording

Many video meeting apps have built-in transcript features. You can also use AI assistants that accept audio file uploads, or speech recognition tools. For better results, use a good microphone and make sure participants don't talk over one another.

## 3. Structure the summary

Give the transcript to the assistant and ask for a clear structure:

"From this meeting transcript, extract the following sections: 1) a 3-sentence overall summary, 2) decisions made, 3) tasks (task, owner, deadline) in a table, 4) open questions. Do not add information that is not in the transcript; mark anything unclear as 'unclear'."

## 4. Verify tasks and owners

AI can misunderstand who took on what, especially with vague statements like "I'll take care of it." Before sharing the task list, check it as someone who attended the meeting.

## 5. Prepare different summaries for different audiences

From the same meeting, you can produce a short status summary for managers, a detailed task list for the team, and an email for the client that contains only the relevant decisions. Make sure the text going to the client does not include internal discussions.

## 6. Split long meetings into sections

For workshops and meetings that run for hours, splitting the transcript into sections and summarizing each one keeps the model from skipping important details.

## 7. Store recordings securely

Transcripts and recordings contain personal data and commercial information. Decide who can access them, delete recordings you no longer need, and check how the tool you use stores data.

## Ready-to-use prompt

"From these meeting notes, write a follow-up email to send to participants: a brief thank-you, decisions, tasks (with owners and dates) and the date of the next meeting. Keep the tone professional and concise."
