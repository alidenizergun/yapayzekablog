Lesson planning, question writing and feedback are among the tasks teachers spend the most time on. AI can produce a quick first draft for these, but the teacher remains responsible for the draft's accuracy, for protecting student data and for final decisions. This guide covers where AI can help, which tools stand out, how to get started and where to stop.

## Keep your expectations realistic

Use is spreading quickly, but the gains are not the same for everyone. According to a Gallup survey of 1,952 K-12 (from kindergarten through 12th grade) educators in the US, 59% of educators used AI tools in the 2025-2026 school year. Of those users, 64% said it freed up time for the most important parts of their work; 7% said AI took up their time, and 29% saw no difference ([Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/715364/teachers-using-say-frees-time-matters-mostost.aspx)). Time savings are based on teachers' self-reports.

According to a news report based on a 2026 TPT survey of 11,500 educators, 80% of respondents use generative AI. Most use is for instructional materials (44%) and idea generation (20%); the share using it for grading is 4%. 93% of teachers say AI cannot replace the materials they create themselves ([THE Journal](https://thejournal.com/articles/2026/04/22/80-of-teachers-are-using-ai-tools-in-the-classroom.aspx)). So widespread use is mainly about drafting and finding ideas.

## Lesson plans, activities and question writing

The safest use of AI is drafting tasks where you set the framework. The more precisely you write the prompt, the more useful the output; for details see the [how to write a good prompt](/rehber/iyi-istem-nasil-yazilir/) guide. Include the following in your prompt:

- Grade level and class length
- Target learning outcome or topic
- Available materials and setting (is there a smartboard, individual work or group work)
- Desired output format (table, step-by-step plan, short activity)

When drafting questions, ask for an answer key in the same chat and verify each question by solving it yourself. Language models can produce made-up information (hallucinations); a wrong answer key costs you dearly in class. Also check activity suggestions for realism: is there enough time, is the material available in your classroom, is it suited to student level?

## Differentiated materials and feedback drafts

Rewriting the same text in simpler language, creating extra practice questions or proposing a challenging task for advanced students are tasks AI does well. MagicSchool's FAQ page says teachers use the platform for planning, assessment, differentiation and communication ([MagicSchool FAQ](https://www.magicschool.ai/faq)). Even so, you need to review differentiated materials yourself, since you know your students; the output may label a student or wrongly assume their level.

For feedback, split the work into two parts:

1. **General part:** Get help from AI with assessment criteria, lists of common mistakes and sentence templates.
2. **Personal part:** Write the student-specific comment yourself.

This split preserves the sincerity of your feedback and means you do not have to upload student homework to a tool. Do not send the draft as is; read it for tone, accuracy and suitability for the student's situation.

## Tools teachers use

**ChatGPT for Teachers.** According to OpenAI's help page, the plan is for verified teachers, staff and administrators at accredited K-12 schools and districts in the US, and it is free until June 2028. The same page notes that pricing may change in the future ([OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/12844995-chatgpt-for-teachers)). Teachers in Türkiye cannot use this plan. The terms for general [ChatGPT](/arac/chatgpt/) versions are separate.

**Gemini and Google Classroom.** At its Bett 2026 announcement, Google said teachers can use Gemini within Classroom to draft assignments and summarize student progress ([Google Blog](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/education/bett-2026-gemini-classroom-updates/)). Availability of [Gemini](/arac/gemini/) features varies by version, country and account type; check with your school's Google Workspace administrator and Google's current pages.

**MagicSchool AI.** A platform for K-12 teachers. According to its FAQ page, it is designed to support teachers rather than replace them, and teachers are expected to review content before sharing it with students ([MagicSchool FAQ](https://www.magicschool.ai/faq)). Before using it, check current plan terms and whether the service is offered in your country on the official page.

**New EBA.** The Ministry of National Education's EBA platform has been renewed with AI support. According to Minister Yusuf Tekin's presentation, the aim is to make it easier for teachers to prepare for and conduct lessons (news report dated October 28, 2026) ([NTV](https://www.ntv.com.tr/egitim/mebden-yapay-zeka-destekli-yeni-eba-1748366)). If you want to use an official channel at your school, check this first.

## Protect student data

The most common mistake is pasting a homework or grade list as is into a chatbot. The general rule: do not enter information that identifies students, such as names, numbers, grades, photos or homework, into general-purpose tools. If necessary, make examples fictional or remove identifying details. For details, see the [protecting personal data](/rehber/yapay-zeka-kullanirken-kisisel-verileri-koruma/) guide.

Plans designed for teachers can make a difference here. OpenAI has said that information shared in ChatGPT for Teachers will not be used to train models and that student data will be protected ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/11/19/openai-chatgpt-for-teachers-k-12-educators.html)). This assurance applies to that plan; for free personal accounts, you need to check data retention and sharing terms yourself.

In Türkiye, you also need to pay attention to the rules of the Ministry of National Education (MEB). According to an official MEB announcement, the Ethics Declaration Form for AI applications in educational institutions has been filled out online via YAZEK since February 2, 2026 ([MEB](https://www.meb.gov.tr/egitimde-yapay-zeka-uygulamalari-etik-kilavuzu-yayimlandi/haber/39496/tr)). There are differing interpretations online about which uses require a declaration; consult the full text of the guide and your school administration. For an institution-level framework, the guide on [AI policy in schools](/rehber/okullarda-yapay-zeka-politikasi/) may also help.

## What AI should not do

- **Should not make final grading decisions.** Grading relies on the teacher's professional judgment. Surveys also show use in this area is low (4% in the TPT survey).
- **Should not make sensitive decisions about students.** Do not hand discipline, guidance or assessments of a student's needs to a model. Models can carry bias.
- **Should not carry unverified information into class.** Check every factual statement, including dates, quotes, formulas and sources.
- **Should not replace the teacher.** MagicSchool's FAQ page also describes the tool as a support and asks that content be reviewed by the teacher.
- **Should not process data that should remain confidential.** Do not enter student and parent information into general tools.

## How to start

Start with a small, low-risk task: a lesson plan draft, a simplified version of a reading text or a ten-question practice set. Check the output yourself before taking it to class, and note what you corrected. After a while, you will see which tasks actually save you time. Some users in the Gallup survey said they saw no difference, so your own measurement matters.

Finally, ask whether your school has an AI policy. Once the accepted tools, the way student data will be protected and the MEB declaration process are clear, both you and your students will be safer.
