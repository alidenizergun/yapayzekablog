Pasting a lab result, prescription or discharge report into a chatbot and saying "explain this to me in plain language" is very easy. But this information is one of the most sensitive types of data in your life, and where it goes and who ends up holding it matters both legally and in practice. This guide does not offer medical advice. It explains the legal status of health data, the risks of uploading data to chatbots, the ChatGPT Health example, and how you can exercise your rights. For any personal situation, consult your doctor or a lawyer before making decisions.

## Why is health data special under KVKK?

According to [KVKK's FAQ document](https://kvkk.gov.tr/SharedFolderServer/CMSFiles/c148901d-cd7f-43ff-ae9a-978fb78fb43c.pdf), personal health data is special-category personal data. Processing this type of data is prohibited as a rule; it can only be processed in the exceptional cases listed in the law or with explicit consent.

The scope is not narrow either. The same source defines health data as any information about a person's physical or mental health, along with information about health services provided. Lab results, past illnesses and medications are listed among the examples. So a medication list you type into a chatbot or a report you upload also falls within this framework.

## Risks of uploading health data to a chatbot

A general-purpose [chatbot](/rehber/yapay-zeka-kullanirken-kisisel-verileri-koruma/) often does not function like a healthcare provider. The confidentiality obligations that apply to a hospital or doctor do not automatically transfer to an app you use on your own. The company's privacy policy and product settings determine which conditions apply.

So the practical principles can be summarized as follows:

- Where possible, remove information that identifies you, such as name, surname, national ID number, address, insurance and patient number.
- Check the settings to see whether the service uses your chat history to develop its models, and turn this off if necessary.
- Remember that anonymization does not remove every risk; report details alone may still point to a specific person.
- Find out in advance where your health data is transferred and how long it is stored.

These steps are not a guarantee but risk-reduction measures. The app may be useful for everyday questions, but do not treat its answers as a substitute for a diagnosis or treatment decision. For the medical side of the issue, see the guide on [using AI for health questions](/rehber/saglik-sorularinda-yapay-zeka-kullanimi/).

## ChatGPT Health: what it offers and what its data policy says

According to [OpenAI's announcement](https://openai.com/index/health-in-chatgpt/), the Health feature is available in the US to users aged 18 and over. Users can connect their medical records and Apple Health data. By default, [ChatGPT](/arac/chatgpt/) asks for permission before using connected record and Apple Health data; this setting can be changed via Settings > Plugins > Health.

[OpenAI's help page](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001036-health-in-chatgpt) says the following about its data policy:

- Connected medical records and Apple Health data, including chats that use this data, are not used to train foundation models or for advertising targeting.
- This rule applies regardless of the chat model training setting.
- Users can disconnect their connected records at any time.
- ChatGPT cannot alter the records held by the provider or Apple Health.

These are the company's own statements. When reading a product's privacy commitment, you should look not only at the text as it stands today but also at how binding that text is.

## What it means to be outside HIPAA protection in the US

When medical records move from a hospital's system to a consumer app, the US health privacy law HIPAA generally does not protect that copy. According to [Paubox](https://www.paubox.com/blog/chatgpt-health-launches-nationwide-but-medical-records-not-hipaa-protected), ChatGPT Health's medical records are not covered by HIPAA; protection rests on OpenAI's own terms. The same article cites Sara Geoghegan of the data protection group EPIC, who says the company can change its terms without regulation.

This does not mean the product is unsafe. But it shows the difference between legal protection and a company commitment: a commitment can change with the company's decision; a legal obligation cannot.

## The situation for users in Turkey

The feature's relevance for users in Turkey is currently limited. According to [Sosyalmedya.org.tr's review](https://sosyalmedya.org.tr/makale/chatgpt-health-herkese-acildi-iddialar-ne-kadar-guvenilir), ChatGPT Health is available only in the US and no official date has been announced for Turkey. So for a user in Turkey, the practical question is less about connecting records and more about what happens to health information they type or upload into the chat.

At the same time, the sources we have do not clearly explain how to file a KVKK claim against a foreign-based company like ChatGPT Health. In such a case, it is wisest to clarify your legal options with a lawyer. For health apps in Turkey, you can also see the guide on [AI in healthcare in Turkey](/rehber/turkiyede-saglikta-yapay-zeka/).

## Patient rights and the KVKK complaint process

If you believe your health data has been unlawfully processed by a hospital, clinic or app company, the process is as follows:

1. **Apply to the data controller first.** According to the [KVKK complaint module guide](https://kvkk.gov.tr/SharedFolderServer/CMSFiles/1624cbad-5ea1-4d73-8519-4a4835eebdb3.pdf), a complaint to the Board cannot be filed without first applying to the data controller.
2. **Wait for the response period.** The data controller must conclude the application within 30 days at the latest and free of charge; rejection decisions must be justified. This framework is set out in the guide and also in [a law firm's summary of methods for exercising rights](https://www.zumbul.av.tr/tr/duyurular/kvkk-tarafindan-ilgili-kisinin-hak-arama-yontemleri-hakkinda).
3. **If necessary, file a complaint with the Board.** According to [Zumbul Hukuk's summary](https://www.zumbul.av.tr/tr/duyurular/kvkk-kapsaminda-veri-sorumlusuna-basvuru-ve-kurul-a-sikayet), a complaint must be filed within 30 days from the date you receive the answer, and in any case within 60 days of the application. If no answer arrives within 30 days, the 60-day limit still applies.
4. **Monitor the outcome.** According to the same sources, if the Board does not conclude within 60 days, the request is deemed rejected. If the Board finds a violation, the data controller has 30 days to comply.

The Board's complaint module only allows the data subject to apply in their own name; applications through a representative, parent or guardian are not possible. Anyone wanting to apply on behalf of a child or a relative they care for should know this detail in particular.

## What to prepare before applying

In your application, briefly describe what data was processed, by which institution, when and how; and state clearly what you are requesting, such as information, correction or deletion. Keep correspondence, screenshots and sending dates, because deadlines run from application and response dates. The method accepted for applications to an institution is specified in its KVKK privacy notice; if you are unsure, check there.

For the obligations of companies and employees on this issue, the guide on [KVKK and AI tools](/rehber/kvkk-ve-yapay-zeka-araclari/) may also be useful.

## Short checklist

- Consider whether sharing health data is really necessary before you do it.
- Remove identifying information; where possible, include only the relevant result or question.
- Check the privacy settings and whether your data is used for training.
- Remember that a company's policy can change, while legal protection is constant.
- Use answers as preparation for questions to take to your doctor, not as a diagnosis or treatment.
- If you believe your rights have been violated, first apply to the data controller in writing and note your deadlines.
