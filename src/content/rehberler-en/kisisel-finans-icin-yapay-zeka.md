Wondering where your salary went by the end of the month is something most of us know well. AI tools can help at this point by drafting budgets, grouping expenses and explaining financial terms in plain language. However, these tools can make mistakes, and there is also a risk that your sensitive data ends up in the wrong hands. This guide explains where to start with AI in personal finance and the limits within which you can use it. It does not include investment advice.

## What does AI help with in personal finance?

In everyday use, AI's contribution to personal finance can be grouped under four headings:

- **Budget drafts:** When you write down your income and fixed expenses, it can suggest a spending plan.
- **Expense grouping:** It can sort transactions with short descriptions into categories such as groceries, transport and bills.
- **Learning concepts:** It can explain terms such as interest, installments, minimum payment and account statement with examples.
- **Easy questions:** Some banking apps let you ask questions about your spending in natural language.

None of these means making decisions for you. AI can often produce an answer that sounds reasonable but is wrong; this is called a hallucination, meaning the generation of made-up information. Compare every output that contains figures with your own records.

## How do you start building a budget?

The safest start is to work only with rounded figures, without entering real account details. You can ask a chatbot for a draft by writing your monthly net income, fixed expenses and the savings goal you want to reach. Example prompt:

> My monthly net income is [amount]. My fixed expenses, such as rent, bills and transport, total [amount]. I want to set aside [amount] at the end of the month. Prepare a simple monthly budget table that groups my expense categories, and state what assumptions you made.

The last sentence matters: asking the model to state its assumptions lets you see where it left gaps in its calculation. Detailed, clear prompts improve results; for more on this, see the [how to write a good prompt](/rehber/iyi-istem-nasil-yazilir/) guide. If you keep the table, checking the calculations in a spreadsheet is also a good habit, and the [AI for Excel and data analysis](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ile-excel-ve-veri-analizi/) guide can help with that.

At the end of the first month, compare the plan with actual spending. Ask the model about any category where you see a deviation, then rearrange the plan based on your own observations.

## Expense categorization and tracking apps

Some budgeting apps sort expenses into categories automatically. Promotional pages and roundup articles circulate high accuracy figures for this categorization, but we could not verify them with an official source. Treat accuracy claims with caution and review the categories yourself during the first few weeks.

One Turkish option, [Cüzdanım](https://cuzdanim.app/), offers free income and expense tracking, spending analysis, and installment and debt management, according to its own website. The site says bank connection is not mandatory and that expenses can be entered manually or by scanning barcodes. The free plan has limits such as 5 expense records and 5 AI usage rights. The app also states that it never asks for internet banking credentials. Another Turkish app, [Akıllı Bütçem](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/ak%C4%B1ll%C4%B1-b%C3%BCt%C3%A7em/id6782052039), highlights on its App Store page the ability to enter expenses in natural language and to record them by photographing receipts. Both pieces of information come from the apps' own promotion; they do not represent an independent evaluation.

When choosing an app, consider the following:

- Does it work without connecting to your bank, including with manual entry?
- Does it explain what data it collects and how it stores it?
- Are the limits of the free plan and the conditions of paid plans clearly stated?

Prices are reported differently on comparison sites, so confirm pricing only on the app's own page.

## AI assistants in banking apps

Banks are also strengthening their assistants with AI. Garanti BBVA's [Ugi assistant](https://www.garantibbva.com.tr/dijital-bankacilik/akilli-asistan-ugi) runs on generative AI. According to the page, it can share card information, list expenses, provide statement information and split eligible single-payment purchases into installments. It also helps with password and limit operations, money transfer fees and contact information updates. The page does not clearly state whether Ugi has a detailed spending analysis tool.

A similar move abroad came from Visa. On July 14, 2026, the company announced its [AI Financial Assistant](https://investor.visa.com/news/news-details/2026/Visa-Introduces-AI-Financial-Assistant-Helping-Banks-Guide-Customers-from-Insight-to-Action/default.aspx) service, which banks can add to their own apps under their own brands. The service offers monthly spending summaries, natural language questions about card transactions, and actions such as locking a card. A pilot for financial institutions in the US is planned to begin in August 2026, followed by a global rollout. The announcement does not name the institutions that will join the pilot, nor does it give pricing or a global timeline; it is not known when it will be offered in Turkey. [According to Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/personal-finance/credit-cards/article/visa-is-launching-an-ai-financial-assistant-for-banking-apps-what-cardholders-should-know-130000385.html), because the assistant runs inside the banking app, account information does not need to be connected to a third-party app.

There is also a [technology news report](https://www.teknotalk.com/google-gemini-banka-hesaplarina-baglanacak-213119/) saying Google will connect its Gemini app to bank and credit card accounts. According to the report, Gemini will be able to show which categories you spend in, find subscriptions and prepare a spending plan for a savings goal; however, it will not be able to make money transfers, payments or investment transactions. The report does not say whether it will be offered in Turkey. When using such features, choosing your bank's own channel assistant first reduces the number of parties with access to your account data. For a general overview of AI in banking, see the [AI in banking](/rehber/bankacilikta-yapay-zeka/) guide.

## Do not share bank passwords or account details

This section covers the most important rule in this guide: do not enter your bank password, internet banking username, one-time verification codes, card number or IBAN into a general-purpose chatbot. If you do not know how an app collects data or where the data is stored, these details are never ones to share.

A practical method: when working with AI and taking information from your own bank statement, remove personal details first. Delete names, account numbers and card information from the descriptions, and keep only the date, category and amount. For a broader framework on protecting personal data, see the [protecting personal data when using AI](/rehber/yapay-zeka-kullanirken-kisisel-verileri-koruma/) guide.

The second risk is fraud. Fake messages that pose as a bank or an AI assistant may ask for passwords and codes. Your bank will not ask for your password by phone or message; if you have doubts, verify through the bank's official app or its known customer service line. The [protecting yourself from AI-enabled fraud](/rehber/yapay-zeka-dolandiriciliklarindan-korunma/) guide is useful for details.

On the regulatory side, work is also ongoing. The [2025-2028 strategic plan](https://www.bddk.org.tr/KurumHakkinda/EkGetir/27?ekId=464) of BDDK (Banking Regulation and Supervision Agency) includes the phrase "procedures and principles regarding the use of artificial intelligence will be determined." The document does not give an effective date, so on its own it is not a guarantee for users. You need to secure yourself through your own habits.

## Do not treat it as investment advice

A budget draft and an investment recommendation are different things. When a chatbot tells you to put a certain amount somewhere, that is not the advice of a licensed expert who knows your personal situation, risk tolerance, and tax or legal circumstances. For questions about which product to invest in and how much, use AI only to explain concepts: what the difference between a term deposit and a fund is, how inflation affects savings, and so on.

Be especially wary of narratives promising 'automatic investment steering.' We covered this topic in the [using AI when investing](/rehber/yatirim-yaparken-yapay-zeka-kullanmak/) guide. As emphasized there, the final word on investment decisions should rest with you and authorized advisors.

## Risks and limits

- **Wrong calculations:** Language models can make errors in summing and rate calculations. Check totals with a calculator or spreadsheet.
- **Outdated information:** Interest rates and campaign terms change often; compare the model's answer with the bank's official page.
- **Inaccurate figures:** Most accuracy and savings-increase claims in promotional texts are not based on independent sources. Do not accept a promise that tries to persuade with numbers without evidence.
- **Data sharing:** User willingness to share data may be increasing. Visa's announcement states that 85% of US users would be willing to share more data with their bank if a suitable AI value proposition were offered. However, willingness does not mean the risk is absent; you still need to control who gets your data and for what purpose.
- **Level of use:** According to the TD Bank 2026 AI Insights Report, as cited by Yahoo Finance, 55% of US adults use AI to manage their finances. This figure is based on a bank report, and widespread use does not prove that the tool is correct.

## A simple plan for the first month

1. Write down your income and fixed expenses in rounded figures and ask a chatbot for a budget draft; do not give personal details.
2. For one month, record your expenses manually or with an app that does not require connecting to your bank.
3. At the end of the month, compare the plan with actual spending and check whether categories were sorted correctly.
4. When you encounter a financial term you do not know, ask the model, then confirm the answer on your bank's or regulators' website.
5. If you use a bank assistant, prefer only the version within your bank's official app.

These steps position AI as a helper that supports your regular work, not as a decision-maker. For real investment, credit and debt decisions, consulting an authorized expert is always the safer choice.
