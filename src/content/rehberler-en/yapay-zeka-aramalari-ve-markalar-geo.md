When you search, you may now come across an AI-written summary above the list of links. Sometimes this summary answers the question on its own; other times it shows several sites as sources. For brands, the question is less about what position they hold in the list and more about whether they are mentioned in that answer and whether the answer brings users to their site. This guide summarizes, in plain language, Google's features, their effect on clicks and what brands can do.

## What is AI search changing?

Google's AI Overviews feature shows a response generated by generative AI at the top of some searches. AI Mode moves search into a chat format, where users can ask questions and continue with follow-up questions. ChatGPT's search feature and services such as [Perplexity](/arac/perplexity/) offer a similar experience, compiling answers from web sources. Most of the figures in this guide relate to Google; solid, independent data for ChatGPT and Perplexity is more limited, and most research on them comes from marketing companies.

The shared outcome for businesses is this: users can get information on the search page or in a chat window and leave. This affects the traffic coming to your site and your brand's first point of contact.

## Effect on click-through rates: what does research say?

One of the most detailed datasets comes from Seer Interactive. The study covers 53 brands, 5.47 million queries and 2.43 billion impressions between January 2025 and February 2026. [According to Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-ctr-recovery-study-475566), the organic click-through rate on searches with AI Overviews fell to 1.3% in December 2025 and rose to 2.4% in February 2026. In the same study, the rate on searches without an AI Overview appears at about 3.3%.

Another study points in the same direction. In an April 2025 study of 300,000 keywords, [Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overviews-reduce-clicks/) found a 34.5% drop in clicks to the top-ranking page when an AI Overview was present. A further finding reported by secondary sources says that in Pew Research's data of 68,879 searches, users clicked traditional results 8% of the time on searches where an AI summary appeared, versus 15% on searches without one ([Presenc](https://presenc.ai/research/ai-overviews-ctr-impact-2026)). We could not verify Pew's own page, so read this figure with caution.

The broader picture also shows a rise in zero-click searches. According to one analysis, using SparkToro and Similarweb data, 68.01% of Google searches ended without a click; in 2024, that figure was 60.45% ([The Blog Timer](https://theblogtimer.com/blog/ai-overviews-ai-mode-click-through-2026)). The same source says Google does not publish its own click data, and the new Search Console report only provides impression information.

There are also places where the numbers do not match. Some compilation pages report the Seer study with different figures, so where possible, check the main news report on which the study was covered. Studies cover different methods and periods, so your own site's data will always be more instructive.

## Does being cited make a difference?

In Seer's data, a page cited within an AI Overview received a click-through rate of about 2.1%, while an uncited page stayed at about 0.9% ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-overviews-ctr-recovery-study-475566)). So being cited increases clicks, but it does not bring them up to the level of searches without a summary. Set realistic goals accordingly: being cited may reduce losses, but it may not eliminate them.

## AI Mode and chat-based search

AI Mode is quickly gaining new capabilities. According to a TechCrunch report dated July 16, 2026, [users can connect some of their apps within AI Mode](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/16/googles-ai-mode-now-lets-you-link-and-interact-with-select-apps/) and take actions such as adding a shopping list to an Instacart cart or saving a playlist to YouTube Music; the feature launched in the US. In November 2026, [temporary chats](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/17/google-ai-mode-temporary-threads/) arrived: these chats are not saved to history, are not used for recommendations, and responses are not personalized.

The implication for brands is that users can ask their question in a chat and finish the task there. In a period when your website is no longer the only entry point, it becomes important that your information can be found accurately and consistently. The fact that temporary chats are not personalized means different users may get similar answers to the same question, but we have no data on how this affects visibility.

## What are GEO and AEO?

GEO (Generative Engine Optimization) is the work of preparing your content so that it will be cited or mentioned in generative AI answers. The concept spread with [an academic paper by Aggarwal and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.09735). AEO (Answer Engine Optimization) describes a similar idea: creating content that answers a question directly. Neither replaces SEO; both are added on top of existing content and technical foundations.

## Where should you start?

Steps you can apply without a large budget:

- **Look first:** Choose 15-20 questions related to your category and ask them in [ChatGPT](/arac/chatgpt/), Perplexity and Google. Record in a table which brands are mentioned, whether you are mentioned and which sources are shown. Repeat the same questions a few weeks later; the answers may change.
- **Track impressions and clicks separately:** If clicks fall while impressions stay stable in Search Console, consider the effect of summaries; however, pinpointing the exact cause is difficult.
- **Answer questions clearly:** Turn the questions customers actually ask into headings on your pages and give the answer in the first paragraph. Keep information such as products, pricing and scope clear and up to date.
- **Add trust signals:** Author information, source citations and consistent brand information (name, address, services) make content easy to verify for both readers and AI.
- **Be present beyond your own site:** AI answers can combine information from different sources. Make sure your brand information is accurate in industry publications, profile pages and review sites.
- **Keep your brand voice:** If you will produce content with AI, see the [brand voice guide](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ile-icerik-uretimi-ve-marka-sesi/); generic text weakens both the reader's engagement and the chance of being cited.

For small teams, the [AI for small businesses](/rehber/kucuk-isletmeler-icin-yapay-zeka/) guide and the article on [uses of AI in marketing](/rehber/pazarlamada-yapay-zeka-kullanim-alanlari/) can offer ideas for building these efforts into your workflow.

## Risks and limits

The biggest limit is uncertainty: from the outside, it is not fully known why a particular source is selected in AI answers. For this reason, view the firm rules and magic formulas proposed for GEO with skepticism. In Turkey too, many pages praise agencies offering GEO services; most of these are promotional in nature. Do not accept offers that promise guaranteed results without asking about the measurement method.

Other risks: some pages report unverified claims about AI Mode from Google announcements (for example, ad placement or user numbers); before making decisions, look for Google's official statement. It is also difficult to measure AI answers: results vary by user, time and query. Drawing conclusions from a single measurement can be misleading.

Finally, when creating content for AI, do not forget the reader. Content that is not clear, accurate and useful to people will not work in the long run for either SEO or GEO. For more examples related to your sector, you can browse the [AI in marketing](/sektor/pazarlama/) page.
