When you paste text into an AI tool to summarize a customer complaint, assess a résumé or have a contract draft corrected, KVKK applies if that text contains personal data. This guide summarizes the guidance of Turkey's Personal Data Protection Authority (KVKK) on generative AI, the rules for transferring data abroad, and what companies should check in practice. It is not legal advice; consult a lawyer for your specific situation.

## What does KVKK's generative AI guidance say?

On November 24, 2025, KVKK published its [15 Questions on Generative AI and Personal Data Protection guide](https://www.srp-legal.com/tr/uretken-yapay-zeka-ve-kisisel-verilerin-korunmasi-rehberi-24-11-2025-tarihinde-kisisel-verileri-koruma-kurumunun-internet-sitesinde-yayimlanmistir/) on the authority's website. The guide says personal data can be processed at every stage of a generative AI system, including training data, prompts and outputs, so the entire system lifecycle must comply with KVKK obligations ([Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/turkey/privacy/ki%C5%9Fisel-verilerin-korunmas%C4%B1-hukukunda-g%C3%BCncel-geli%C5%9Fmeler)).

In practice, this means the issue is not only what a model was trained on. The information you write in a prompt, and any real person's information that appears in a model's response, can both be considered data processing.

## What legal basis is required?

According to the guide, personal data processing in generative AI must rely on one of the conditions in Article 5 (general data) or Article 6 (special category data) of KVKK. For special category data, contract performance and legitimate interest are not accepted as bases ([SRP Legal](https://www.srp-legal.com/tr/uretken-yapay-zeka-ve-kisisel-verilerin-korunmasi-rehberi-24-11-2025-tarihinde-kisisel-verileri-koruma-kurumunun-internet-sitesinde-yayimlanmistir/)). This is why it is worth thinking twice before entering categories such as health data into any tool.

KVKK's earlier Recommendations on the Protection of Personal Data in the Field of Artificial Intelligence also suggest conducting a privacy impact assessment when risk is high, anonymizing data if the result can be reached without processing personal data, defining the data controller and data processor roles at the start of a project, and giving individuals a right to object ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=6dbf3e00-0500-420d-9b69-41fb99b5f984)). In addition, KVKK's Principle Decision No. 2026/347, dated February 18, 2026, stresses that privacy notices and explicit consent texts must be drafted separately ([Halil Bakırcı Hukuk](https://halilbakirci.av.tr/kvkk-yurt-disina-veri-aktarimi-arizi-haller-madde-9-6-2026/)).

According to the guide, publicly available data should not be assumed to be freely usable for AI training ([Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/turkey/privacy/ki%C5%9Fisel-verilerin-korunmas%C4%B1-hukukunda-g%C3%BCncel-geli%C5%9Fmeler)).

## Transferring data abroad: why does it matter?

Most popular AI tools run on servers outside Turkey. According to the guide, using an AI system hosted outside Turkey counts as a transfer of personal data abroad ([Legal 500](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/turkey/privacy/ki%C5%9Fisel-verilerin-korunmas%C4%B1-hukukunda-g%C3%BCncel-geli%C5%9Fmeler)). So sending a prompt containing personal data to a foreign service may fall under the regime in Article 9 of KVKK.

Law No. 7499, published on March 12, 2024, changed this regime. Under the [new regulation](https://abk.av.tr/kvkk-yurt-disi-veri-aktarimi-2026/), there are three steps for transfers: an adequacy decision, appropriate safeguards (standard contracts and binding corporate rules) and occasional cases. Explicit consent is no longer the only option for every transfer. The standard contract forms were published by the authority on July 10, 2024.

Occasional transfers under Article 9(6) can only be applied to exceptional cases that are not regular or continuous ([Halil Bakırcı Hukuk](https://halilbakirci.av.tr/kvkk-yurt-disina-veri-aktarimi-arizi-haller-madde-9-6-2026/)). Relying on this exception for an AI tool you use every day may not be a sound plan. Some legal commentators debate whether a foreign provider remotely accessing data counts as a transfer; on this point, check the official text and consult your legal adviser.

## Employees entering data into tools: shadow AI

KVKK's document on the Use of Generative AI Tools in the Workplace defines shadow AI as employees using generative AI tools in work processes without the organization's knowledge, approval or control. The document recommends creating a corporate usage policy, managing access to tools through rules and providing employees with awareness training ([Erdem & Erdem](https://www.erdem-erdem.av.tr/bilgi-bankasi/is-yerlerinde-uretken-yapay-zeka-araclarinin-kullanimi-baslikli-kisisel-verileri-koruma-kurumu-duyurusu-yayimlandi)).

For example, an employee copying a customer complaint into ChatGPT from a personal account may fall within this scope. Because the company bears responsibility as the data controller, its audit and policy obligations become more demanding in such cases. An employee's individual decision is unlikely to be a useful defense for the business.

## What the numbers show

We could not find a Turkey-specific employee survey; the available data comes from abroad. In a survey conducted for Zapier by Centiment from May 13 to 21, 2026, 1,005 US employees at companies with at least 500 staff participated. [According to this survey](https://kvia.com/stacker-cybersecurity-privacy/2026/09/23/79-of-workers-have-entered-sensitive-info-like-logins-into-ai/), 89% of workers had entered company data into AI, and 79% had shared sensitive information such as HR data, login credentials, API keys, or customer or employee personal data. 36% were not fully sure what could be shared, and 50% said their workplace ignores its AI restrictions.

These results should not be transferred directly to Turkey. Still, they suggest that uncertainty and the tendency to bypass rules are problems that can be reduced with a well-written policy and training.

## What safeguards should you look for in enterprise plans?

Enterprise and business plans generally offer different data terms from personal accounts, but these terms vary by provider and by plan. Rather than accepting general assurances, read the contract and the provider's current documentation. Get written answers to the following questions:

- Are your inputs and outputs used for model training, and can this be disabled at the account level?
- How long is data retained, and how are deletion requests handled?
- In which countries is data processed, and which sub-processors are used?
- Does the provider act as a data processor, and is this role written into the contract?
- Are admin controls, access management and audit logging available?
- Which mechanism (for example, a standard contract) can be used for transfers abroad?

A business plan does not remove your obligations for transfer bases and privacy notices; these remain with you as the data controller. To set up your company's overall framework, see our guide to [company AI policy](/rehber/sirket-ici-yapay-zeka-politikasi/).

## Checklist for companies

1. **Take an inventory.** Which departments use which tools, and what types of data do they enter? Include shadow usage.
2. **Prepare an approved tool list.** Clearly state which tools may be used and under what conditions.
3. **Look for all three conditions together.** A law firm's [policy template](https://zamana.com.tr/wiki/temeller/sirket-ici-politika/) recommends requiring, for customer data, a privacy notice, an Article 5 processing condition and an Article 9 transfer basis together. If none of these are in place, do not enter data even into an approved tool.
4. **Separate special category data.** For categories such as health data, contracts and legitimate interest are not valid bases; anonymize where possible. For the health side, see our guide on [health data and AI privacy](/rehber/saglik-verisi-ve-yapay-zeka-gizlilik/).
5. **Conduct an impact assessment.** If risk is high, follow the recommendation to carry out a privacy impact assessment and define responsibility roles at the start of the project.
6. **Separate privacy notices and consent texts.** Update the texts when a new processing purpose emerges.
7. **Provide training.** Explain with examples what must not be entered; our guide to [AI training for employees](/rehber/calisanlara-yapay-zeka-egitimi/) can be a useful starting point.
8. **Review contracts.** Clarify the provider's terms on data processing, retention, deletion and sub-processors.

## Limits and points to watch

Some of what has been written on this topic relies on law firm summaries; do not make decisions without comparing them against the official text. In particular, the publication date of KVKK's workplace document appears differently across sources, so check the official date on the authority's website. KVKK's approach in this area and the rules on transfers abroad may be updated.

For more practical steps as an everyday user, see our guide to [protecting personal data when using AI](/rehber/yapay-zeka-kullanirken-kisisel-verileri-koruma/), and for the broader framework, visit our [KVKK and AI tools](/rehber/kvkk-ve-yapay-zeka-araclari/) page. The safest starting point is knowing in writing what you are entering, into which tool, and on what legal basis.
