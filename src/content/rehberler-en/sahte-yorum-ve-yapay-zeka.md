Writing product reviews with generative AI has become technically very easy. From a legal and commercial standpoint, however, it is becoming increasingly risky. This guide summarizes the US Federal Trade Commission (FTC) rule, the regulations under discussion around Türkiye's Ministry of Trade, Amazon's detection efforts, and the points e-commerce sellers should watch for. This is not legal advice; consult a lawyer for a specific situation.

## What is the problem and why is it growing?

Reviews and ratings are often the strongest social proof in purchasing decisions. Large language models (LLMs) can write convincing reviews in the voice of someone who never used the product. This lowers the cost of producing fake reviews. Regulators and platforms are responding by tightening the rules. Below, you will find what these responses contain.

## US: The FTC's fake review rule

The FTC [announced a final rule on August 14, 2024](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) that bans the sale and purchase of fake reviews and testimonials. The rule provides for civil money penalties for fake reviews. This means it covers not only the person who writes a review, but also anyone who sells or buys one.

The rule's text does not mention artificial intelligence. However, according to [an analysis by the law firm Alston & Bird](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2024/10/ftc-issues-final-rule-on-fake-reviews-testimonials), the FTC's explanatory statement clearly states that reviews generated with AI are also covered. Therefore, using a model to write reviews in the names of customers who never used the product falls within the behaviors targeted by this rule.

## What has the FTC done in practice?

In December 2025, the FTC [sent warning letters about fake reviews and vacated the Rytr order under Operation AI Comply](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-us/insights/publications/2025/12/ftc-warning-letters-ai-consumer-reviews). According to DLA Piper, the identities of the companies that received the letters were not disclosed. The letter template cites, as an example violation, paying employees for 5-star reviews. This means the rule is not limited to AI-generated text; paid and directed reviews also carry risk.

## Türkiye: Regulations on reviews

The basic procedures and principles for publishing consumer reviews are set out in Article 28/B of the Regulation on Commercial Advertising and Unfair Commercial Practices. According to the framework reported in the news, [only people who purchased the relevant good or service can post reviews](https://www.haberler.com/ekonomi/ticaret-bakanligi-sahte-urun-yorumlarina-yonelik-20286019-haberi/). Reviews are published after the necessary checks, without distinguishing between positive and negative ones.

According to [a report by Gazete Arena](https://www.gazetearena.com/e-ticarette-sahte-yorum-donemi-bitiyor), the new rules took effect on August 1, 2026. The same report highlights the following points:

- Published reviews must remain accessible for at least one year.
- Sellers and platforms cannot remove a review from publication just because it is negative.
- The Ministry of Trade (Ticaret Bakanlığı) said the Advertising Board (Reklam Kurulu) regularly reviews ratings on e-commerce platforms.

Platforms are also [prohibited](https://www.eniyihaberler.com/ekonomi/e-ticarette-sahte-yorumlara-gecit-yok-bakanlik-erisim-engeli-getirdi-70307h) from sorting reviews in a way that steers consumer choices in a particular direction. According to the same report, following complaints, the Advertising Board blocked access to numerous websites that sell followers and reviews or provide artificial engagement. The exact number of blocked sites has not been disclosed.

Most of this information is based on news reports. Before implementing anything, verify the current text of the regulation through Ministry of Trade announcements and the official legislation.

## Penalties: What is known and what is not?

In the US, the official announcement states that the rule provides for civil money penalties. In Türkiye, the general framework consists of administrative fines under Law No. 6502 on the Protection of the Consumer. The Ministry [announced that penalties under this law were increased by 25.49% starting January 1, 2026](https://tuketici.ticaret.gov.tr/haberler/6502-sayili-tuketicinin-korunmasi-hakkinda-kanun-kapsaminda-uygulanan-idari-para-cezalari-1-ocak-2026-tarihinden-itibaren-25-49-oraninda-arttirildi). The same announcement lists a penalty of 3,922 TL per contract for failing to remove an unfair term from a contract.

Note: this amount is not a penalty specific to fake reviews; it is one of the law's general penalties. This guide cannot specify which provision would apply to fake reviews or what the amount would be. Clarify this with a lawyer based on your specific situation.

## How do platforms catch fake reviews?

Amazon said it [blocked hundreds of millions of suspected fake reviews before they were published in 2025](https://www.aboutamazon.com/news/policy-news-views/amazon-trustworthy-shopping-experience-report-2025). According to the company, reviews are analyzed before publication using thousands of data points and data accumulated since 1995. This means a machine learning-based assessment that uses account and history data in addition to the review text.

These figures are the company's own statements. The details of the method and its accuracy rate are not clear from this announcement. Think of platform detection as a screening of reviews before they go live. It does not mean every fake review will be caught.

## Risks and a checklist for sellers

Taken together, the rules point to the following risk areas for sellers:

- **Do not buy reviews.** Services that sell reviews and followers are targeted in both the US and Türkiye; in Türkiye, such sites may face access bans.
- **Do not collect paid 5-star ratings.** The example violation in the FTC warning letter template is exactly this.
- **Do not have reviews written with AI.** Text produced in the voice of someone who never used the product falls within the rule's scope.
- **Do not delete negative reviews.** In Türkiye, removing a review just because it is negative is prohibited; reviews must remain accessible for one year.
- **Do not steer the ranking.** Arranging reviews in a way that pushes consumers toward a particular choice is prohibited.
- **Ask real customers for reviews.** A polite reminder after purchase is not a problem, as long as it does not steer the review.

## Where should you start?

The first step is to review your current review processes. If an agency or employee collects reviews for you, check whether they are paid and whether the people writing the reviews are actual buyers. Then make sure negative reviews have not been deleted and that review records are being kept. Finally, remember that rules change quickly: monitor the Ministry of Trade and FTC announcements regularly.
