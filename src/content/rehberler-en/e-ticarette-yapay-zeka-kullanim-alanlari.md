AI in e-commerce is no longer just the business of large platforms. It now reaches the everyday work of small sellers, from product copy to return correspondence. This guide covers where it can help, how to get started and where you need to be careful. Many of the figures cited come from surveys, news reports and company announcements. Because they rest on different definitions, be cautious when comparing them.

## What does research say about adoption?

According to a report on the state of e-commerce in Turkey by the Ministry of Trade, about 84% of e-commerce companies have used AI to generate text and images. Content creation is the most common use case, while legal uncertainty is cited as the biggest obstacle. A news report says the report was published on May 15, 2026 ([Sözcü](https://www.sozcu.com.tr/e-ticarette-yapay-zeka-kullanimi-yayginlasiyor-en-buyuk-engel-hukuki-belirsizlik-p319853)). Another news article based on the same report says 75% of businesses actively use AI ([Yalın Haberler](https://www.yalinhaberler.com/haber/280221/turkiyede-e-ticaret-isletmelerinin-yapay-zeka-kullanim-orani-yuzde-75-seviyesine-ulasti.html)). The two figures may rest on different definitions, so do not use one in place of the other.

According to TÜİK (Turkish Statistical Institute) 2026 AI statistics, 14% of enterprises use at least one AI technology, up from 7.5% in 2025. The most common purpose among enterprises that use AI is marketing or sales, at 51%. The share of individuals using generative AI rose from 19.2% in 2025 to 37.6% in 2026 ([Ticaret Gazetesi](https://ticaretgazetesi.com.tr/2026/10/02/yapay-zeka-kullanimi-iki-kat-artti/)). In other words, both sellers and buyers are getting used to these tools. However, these figures do not guarantee results for your business. Your own small tests will be a better guide.

## Product titles and descriptions

This is the easiest place to start: a repetitive, high-volume task where you can see the results quickly. You can give a generative AI tool the product features, target audience and desired tone, and ask it for a draft title and description. When writing the prompt, provide the real details yourself, such as measurements, materials and usage information. For guidance on how to do this, see our [guide to writing a good AI prompt](/rehber/iyi-istem-nasil-yazilir/).

There are also tools built specifically for this task. For example, MerchantFloor announced an AI product listing agent for Shopify stores. According to the company's press release dated November 5, 2026, the tool generates a description from a product photo and a few notes, along with an SEO title, meta description, frequently asked questions and image alt text. The free trial includes 25 credits (five listings), paid credit packs start at $20, and the company estimates the cost at about $1 per listing ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/05/3396739/0/en/the-5-minute-product-listing-merchantfloor-launches-ai-agent-for-small-shopify-stores.html)). These are the company's own claims, so test the tool on your own products before buying.

The main risk here is hallucination. The model can write a fluent sentence about a feature your product does not have, or give a wrong measurement. Before publishing, check every detail against your supplier documents.

## Product images

On the visual side, AI can be used to create draft images such as white-background product photos, model shots and lifestyle images. MerchantFloor's announcement lists these features as well. The same press release notes that images from the free trial carry watermarks. For a detailed workflow, see our [guide to AI product photography](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ile-urun-fotografi/).

The key rule for images is this: what you promise the buyer must match the product they receive. If a generated image shows a different color, texture or size than the real product, returns and complaints will increase. The legal status of AI-generated images can be uncertain, so consult a legal expert whenever you are unsure.

## Customer questions, returns and support

Frequently asked questions about shipping times, size charts and return conditions are well suited to a chatbot. According to a Digital Commerce 360 report based on FedEx data, 37% of sellers already use AI for returns, and 51% plan to. Among consumers, 20% turn to AI chatbots for return information. Of those who use them for support, 53% prefer a human agent and have low trust in the tools ([Digital Commerce 360](https://www.digitalcommerce360.com/2026/01/22/ecommerce-trends-returns-charges-ai/)).

This data points to a balanced setup: the bot should answer only based on your written policies, should not make up answers when it does not know, and should hand the customer over to a human if they ask. When entering personal data such as order numbers, addresses and phone numbers into any tool, follow the precautions in our [guide to protecting personal data when using AI](/rehber/yapay-zeka-kullanirken-kisisel-verileri-koruma/).

## Review analysis

Customer reviews are valuable for seeing which complaints recur for a given product. However, this guide does not rely on a numerical finding in this area. In practice, the process is simple: export the last few hundred reviews, remove names and contact details, and ask an AI tool to group the reviews by topic (shipping, quality, sizing, packaging) and show example sentences from each topic.

Do not accept the summary blindly. Compare a few topics against the original reviews, since the tool may misclassify irony or short comments. Also, have the AI draft replies to customers, but read them yourself before sending.

## Advertising, campaigns and visibility in AI search

AI increasingly runs behind the automation on advertising platforms. According to Amazon's Accelerate 2026 announcement, the same AI is used in ad campaigns. The company says that, based on early results, first sales in new campaigns arrive 40% faster. Amazon's free Ads Data Manager tool also connects the seller's own customer data to campaigns. The same announcement introduces Seller Assistant, a free chat assistant for sellers, and says an add-on for Claude and Amazon Quick is available in the US ([Amazon Selling Partners](https://sellingpartners.aboutamazon.com/accelerate-2026-recap)). These are the company's own statements, and the source does not say whether these tools are available in Turkey. For the broader picture, our [guide to AI on advertising platforms](/rehber/reklam-platformlarinda-yapay-zeka/) is also useful.

There is also a new channel where buyers find products by asking AI. In a study by Narron, an İTÜ Çekirdek startup, researchers examined whether ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity recommended 99 Turkish e-commerce brands across 50 unbranded queries. Trendyol ranked first with 17.52%, and Hepsiburada ranked second with 14.96%. Of the 99 brands, 32 were almost never recommended in the answers. According to the study, AI responses reward category expertise, content depth and trust signals more than brand size ([Gündem Ekonomet](https://mobil.gundemekonometre.com/haber/yapay-zek-turkiye-de-hangi-e-ticaret-markalarini-oneriyor/12051/)). The implication for small sellers is that a clear category focus and detailed product content benefit both customers and these tools. For more on this topic, see our [guide to AI search and brands](/rehber/yapay-zeka-aramalari-ve-markalar-geo/).

## A practical starting workflow for SMEs (small and medium-sized enterprises)

Do not automate everything at once. You could try this sequence:

1. **Choose one product group.** Start with 10-20 of your best-selling or most-asked-about products.
2. **Draft product copy.** Give the tool real product data and correct the output yourself. Measure how many minutes you save using your own records.
3. **Compile frequently asked questions.** List the questions and answers that come up most often in customer messages, and make the list available to both the chatbot and your team.
4. **Summarize reviews monthly.** Use the results to improve your product pages and supplier communication.
5. **Start small with advertising.** Try the platform's built-in suggestions, keep the budget limited and compare results with your own sales data.

If you sell on a marketplace, also look at the platform's own tools. According to Trendyol CEO Erdem İnan at Slush'D Istanbul (October 2026), sellers can export in 10 languages using Trendyol LLM. The platform also offers Ortak, a digital assistant for sellers, along with stock and product design analysis based on demand data ([Analiz53](https://analiz53.com/haber/trendyol-yapay-zeka-ile-e-ticaretin-gelecegini-sekillendiriyor-164967)). These are the company's own descriptions, so check what is available in your seller panel. For a broader framework, see our [guide to AI for marketplace sellers](/rehber/pazaryeri-saticilari-icin-yapay-zeka/).

## Risks and limits

- **Accuracy:** Generated text and summaries can be wrong. Always have a person check binding information such as prices, measurements, warranties and return conditions.
- **Legal uncertainty:** According to the news report based on the Ministry of Trade data above, this is the biggest obstacle reported by the sector. When you are unsure about advertising, consumer disclosure or image use, consult an expert.
- **Personal data:** Remove identifying information from customer messages and reviews before giving them to a tool.
- **Trust in figures:** Industry reports and company announcements often highlight the benefits of the product they describe. Do not treat them as a substitute for your own experience.
- **Cost:** For credit-based or subscription tools, calculate the unit cost, meaning the cost per listing or per message, from the first week.

In short, AI can save time on repetitive content and support tasks in e-commerce, but the final decision and responsibility remain with the seller.
