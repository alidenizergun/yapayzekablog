AI companies announce new models every month, and each claims to be number one on some test. In this confusion, the way to choose the right model is to start from your own needs rather than from benchmark tables. The criteria below apply to both individual users and companies.

## 1. Define the task you need done

Models are strong at different things. You can roughly split tasks into these groups:

- **Everyday messaging, summaries and brainstorming:** Almost all current models do these well. Speed and ease of use matter more.
- **Coding and technical work:** Prefer models that stand out on coding tests and that integrate well with developer tools (code editors, terminal tools).
- **Math, analysis and multi-step problems:** Reasoning models that "think" before answering are noticeably more successful, but they are slower and more expensive.
- **Long documents and codebases:** Look for models with a large context window.
- **Images, audio and video:** You need multimodal models or tools specialized for the job.

## 2. Calculate the cost

In chat apps, most companies offer a free tier and a monthly subscription. If you will connect a model to your own application via API, pricing is usually set per million tokens, with separate prices for input and output. Large models can be several times, or even dozens of times, more expensive than small ones. Choosing a small, fast model for many simple tasks and a large model for a few hard tasks is usually the most economical approach.

## 3. Consider speed and usage volume

Response time is critical in a chatbot talking to a customer in real time; it doesn't matter for a report-generation job that runs overnight. Fast models (those offered under names like "flash," "mini" and "haiku") are designed for high-volume work.

## 4. Check the privacy and data policy

For work involving personal data or trade secrets, find out where your data is processed, how long it is stored and whether it is used to train the model. If data must not leave your environment, running an open-weight model on your own servers is an option.

## 5. Look at Turkish-language performance

Most models understand Turkish well, but writing quality, idioms and terminology vary from model to model. Also, because Turkish text can consume more tokens than English, API costs may be slightly higher. Test with your own Turkish texts before deciding.

## 6. Run your own test

Benchmark results give you an idea, but they don't measure your work. Pick 5-10 samples from your real tasks (an email, a report summary, a piece of code) and give the same prompt to two or three models. Compare the results on accuracy, language quality, speed and cost. This small test is the most reliable way to choose.

## Quick decision list

- If the task is hard and mistakes are costly: the most powerful model or a reasoning model.
- If the task is simple and repeated often: a fast, cheap model.
- If data must not leave your environment: an enterprise contract or an open-weight model on your own servers.
- If you work with long documents: a large context window.
- If you are unsure: compare two models on your own samples.
