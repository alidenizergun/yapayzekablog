Advertising platforms have shifted much of campaign decision-making away from advertisers and onto their own AI systems in recent years. This guide focuses on Google Ads' Performance Max (PMax) campaign type: how it works, when it makes sense, and how you can keep control despite the automation. We rely only on information found on the platform's own pages. For broader uses of AI in marketing, see our [AI use cases in marketing](/rehber/pazarlamada-yapay-zeka-kullanim-alanlari/) guide and the [marketing industry page](/sektor/pazarlama/).

## What is Performance Max?

According to Google's definition, [Performance Max](https://business.google.com/tr/ad-solutions/performance-max/) is an AI-based campaign type that lets advertisers reach all Google Ads channels through a single campaign. These channels include YouTube, the Display Network, Search, Discover, Gmail and Maps.

In traditional campaigns, you set up each channel separately and make separate bidding and targeting decisions for each. With PMax, you create a single campaign and provide your goal and assets; the system decides where, to whom and in what format the ad will be shown.

## What does the AI decide?

According to Google, Google's AI makes the bidding, budget, audience and creative decisions in PMax. In other words, your job is to feed the system the right inputs: the campaign goal, budget, texts, images and videos.

Material quality is the critical point here. Google [recommends providing high-quality text, images and videos](https://business.google.com/tr/ad-solutions/performance-max/) so the AI can produce effective combinations. Because the system combines your assets for different channels and formats, weak or similar-looking content means weak combinations. If you use generative AI to prepare your materials, our guide on [maintaining brand voice](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ile-icerik-uretimi-ve-marka-sesi/) may help.

## Settings that are on by default

According to Google's [frequently asked questions about PMax](https://business.google.com/us/accelerate/resources/articles/answering-your-top-questions-about-performance-max/), keywordless AI targeting is always on in PMax. Text customization and final URL expansion are also on by default.

In practical terms, this means:

- **Keywordless targeting:** You do not manage the campaign with a keyword list. The system targets based on the materials you provide and conversion data it learns from.
- **Text customization:** Ad text may be adapted by the system. Before going live, check whether this setting fits your brand voice.
- **Final URL expansion:** This allows the system to direct traffic to pages other than the one you specified. Monitor which pages are being used; if traffic is going to a page you don't want, review this setting.

You can launch a campaign without changing the default settings, but knowing they are on and making a deliberate decision about them is your responsibility.

## Controls Google has added

The most common criticism of automation is that it is hard to see what is happening. Google announced on its [new features and announcements page](https://support.google.com/google-ads/announcements/9048695?hl=en) that it would add goal and visual controls to PMax in 2025. Check the help center and your own Google Ads interface to see which controls have been enabled in your account and at what stage. Availability may vary by account and region.

## When does it work?

The commentary in this section is drawn from general logic rather than official documentation; test it with your own data.

PMax may make more sense under these conditions:

- Your goal is clear and measurable (such as sales or form submissions), and you are passing conversions to your account accurately.
- You want to appear across several Google channels but do not have the time to manage a separate campaign for each one.
- You can produce quality materials in different formats (text, images, video).

Be cautious in these situations:

- Your conversion tracking is incomplete or faulty, because the system learns from the wrong signals.
- You want to appear only on a specific channel or for specific keywords; in that case, campaign types with more granular control may be a better fit.
- Every sentence of your brand voice matters and you are not comfortable with automatic text adaptation.

## Tips for staying in control

1. **Write the goal in one sentence.** Before launching the campaign, clarify what you are optimizing for. A vague goal produces vague results.
2. **Diversify your materials.** Instead of small variations on the same message, provide different angles, different images and different video lengths.
3. **Review the default settings one by one.** Text customization and final URL expansion are on by default; before accepting them, assess them against your brand and your site structure.
4. **Plan the test period deliberately.** Automation systems need data to learn. Checking results and changing settings frequently in the first few days makes it harder to understand what the system is doing.
5. **Review results by channel and by page.** Even if the total figure looks good, you need to know where your budget went.
6. **Don't forget data privacy.** When using audience and conversion data, see our guide on [personalization and KVKK (Turkey's Personal Data Protection Law)](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ile-kisisellestirme-ve-kvkk/) for obligations on processing personal data.

## Meta Advantage+ and TikTok Smart+

Meta's Advantage+ and TikTok's Smart+ automation tools also leave part of advertisers' decision-making to the platform's systems. Because this guide is limited to information that can be verified through official platform documentation, it does not go into the detailed operating logic, budget or targeting options of these two tools. Before using them, read the relevant platform's own help center; in particular, check which settings are automatic, which ones you can change, and what the performance reports show and do not show. The questions above for PMax (goal, materials, default settings, reporting) are also a good starting checklist when evaluating these tools.

## Risks and limits

Automation saves time but may require you to sacrifice some transparency. Even when the system appears to produce good results, you may not be able to fully see why. For this reason, rather than relying solely on one platform's report, compare results with your own sales or customer data where possible. Also, platform features and default settings change over time, so do not apply the information in this guide without verifying it in your own account and in current help pages.
