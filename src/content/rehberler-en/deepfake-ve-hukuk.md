AI-generated fake images and voices can make it appear that a person said things they never said or did things they never did. This guide summarizes the legal areas under which deepfake content may fall in Turkey, the routes victims can take, regulatory preparations, and the EU's labeling rule. The text is for general information only and is not legal advice. Consult a lawyer about any specific case. If you want to understand the technical side, start with the [how to spot a deepfake](/rehber/deepfake-nasil-anlasilir/) guide.

## Is a deepfake banned by itself?

According to a law firm's assessment, [a deepfake is not illegal on its own](https://aeylegal.com/deepfakein-hukuki-degerlendirmesi/); a crime can arise depending on consent, purpose and how it is distributed. A fiction made with a person's consent, that misleads no one and infringes no right, is not considered a crime. The problem begins when the content uses someone's identity without permission, damages their reputation, or is used to deceive, threaten or harm a person.

## Which provisions of the Turkish Penal Code may apply?

The Turkish Penal Code (TCK) has no standalone offense called deepfake. According to a law office's article, depending on the nature of the act, [insult, violation of privacy, unlawful dissemination of personal data and other provisions](https://beratozkan.av.tr/deepfake-ve-yapay-zeka-ile-islenen-suclar/) may apply. For this reason, two pieces of content produced with the same technology can have entirely different legal consequences.

A few points stand out:

- **Debate over private-life images:** It is debated whether an artificially produced content counts as an image of private life. By contrast, because face, voice and biometric features are considered personal data, they may fall under TCK Article 136. The same source reports this view.
- **Child victims:** If the victim is a child, [TCK Article 226 (obscenity, use of children)](https://serh.org/article/yapay-zek-ile-uretilen-ciplak-goruntuler-grok-ornegi-uzerinden-kisilik-haklari-ve-platform-sorumlulugunun-hukuki-analizi) may also come into play.
- **Proposed new offense:** In legal literature, a new offense defining the act of producing and distributing sexual deepfakes without consent is reportedly being proposed. This is a proposal, not a provision currently in force.

Be careful about penalty ranges. Law firms' blog posts give different year ranges for TCK Articles 134 and 136. For this reason, we do not list the figures here; check current articles against the official legislation text.

## Personality rights and personal data

Alongside criminal proceedings, there is also a private-law route. Under Article 24 of the Turkish Civil Code (TMK), an attack on personality rights made without consent, a superior private or public interest, or legal authority is considered [unlawful](https://aeylegal.com/deepfakein-hukuki-degerlendirmesi/). A moral damages lawsuit can be filed for violation of personality rights. Sources cite TMK Articles 24-25 and Turkish Code of Obligations (TBK) Article 58 on this point.

For the personal data dimension, you may also review the guide on [protecting personal data](/rehber/yapay-zeka-kullanirken-kisisel-verileri-koruma/) when using AI. According to one assessment, the current Personal Data Protection Law (KVKK, Turkey's Personal Data Protection Authority) contains no explicit provision specific to AI-generated content. In practice, this means general provisions must be interpreted.

## Which routes can victims take?

The process generally proceeds in the following order, but every case has different circumstances, so acting together with a lawyer is the safest approach.

1. **Collect evidence.** Save the link (URL) to the content, a screenshot and the publication date. According to sources, a record can also be secured through a notary or electronic record-keeping (e-tespit).
2. **File a complaint with the platform.** Platforms' own complaint mechanisms can be a first step toward quick removal.
3. **Request an access ban under personality rights.** Under Article 9 of Law No. 5651, [an application is first made to the content provider](https://bilalalyar.av.tr/internet-yayin-kisilik-haklari/); if the application is rejected or there is no response within 24 hours, the matter goes to a criminal judgeship of peace.
4. **File a criminal complaint.** The victim can apply to the chief public prosecutor's office, stating that the content is fake and was produced without their consent.
5. **Consider a compensation lawsuit.** Moral damages can be based on TMK and TBK provisions.

There are also some limits. Sources give conflicting information about the respondent for an application under Article 9/A of Law No. 5651 on privacy; for this reason, clarify the application route with a lawyer. The period for an insult complaint is given as six months (TCK Article 73), but sources do not agree on the statute of limitations for moral damages. Acting without losing time is the most practical way to resolve these uncertainties. Against access-ban decisions, there are also remedies: an objection to the criminal judgeship of peace and an individual application to the Constitutional Court.

An access ban alone may not be enough. Sources emphasize that content can move to other accounts, platforms or foreign websites. For fake advertisements used for fraud, the guide on [protecting yourself from AI scams](/rehber/yapay-zeka-dolandiriciliklarindan-korunma/) is also helpful.

## Regulatory preparations in Turkey

Turkey has no law in force that regulates AI in all its aspects. According to a law firm's article, the TBMM (Turkish Grand National Assembly) Artificial Intelligence Research Commission also proposed a Turkey-specific law in its draft report completed in January 2026. You can find the general picture in the guide on [AI in Turkish law](/rehber/turkiyede-hukukta-yapay-zeka/).

The most concrete initiative targeting fake content is a bill submitted to the TBMM in January 2026 that proposes amendments to the KVKK (Law No. 6698). According to TBMM records, the bill's registration date is January 9, 2026; it is awaiting action in the Justice Committee (lead) and the Digital Media Committee (secondary). [The record shows no enactment or General Assembly action](https://www.tbmm.gov.tr/Yasama/KanunTeklifi/90b549a1-3c01-4789-a768-019b9e131772). The main regulations the bill proposes are:

- An administrative fine of 5% of the previous year's revenue for platforms that allow such content to be shared.
- A requirement to add the label "Yapay Zekâ Tarafından Üretilmiştir" (roughly, "Produced by AI") to AI-generated fake content. Violations of this obligation [would be punished under TCK Article 217/A](https://sahinhukukdanismanlik.av.tr/yapay-zekaya-iliskin-kanun-teklifi-tbmmye-sunuldu/).
- A new paragraph to be added to Law No. 5651 requiring access bans or removal of such content within six hours at the latest.

Here is an important warning: the six-hour deadline appears only in the bill text, not in force. Some blog posts present this period as if it were an existing regulation. A comprehensive AI Bill submitted in September 2025 also envisaged labeling of deepfake content and administrative fines. The AI Bill (2/2234) submitted in June 2024 appears in TBMM records as being in committee. So none of these is currently an operative rule. For the internal side at companies, the guide on [company AI policy](/rehber/sirket-ici-yapay-zeka-politikasi/) offers a starting point.

## EU labeling obligation: Article 50

Article 50 of the EU AI Act began to apply on August 2, 2026. Users (deployers) who produce or edit deepfake content must disclose that the content was generated by AI. [According to DLA Piper's summary](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/08/the-eus-new-guidelines-on-ai-transparency), the Commission published a non-binding guideline on Article 50 on July 20, 2026.

Machine-readable marking is also envisaged for providers. [A transition period until December 2, 2026](https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/eu-finalises-transparency-rules-for-ai-generated-content) applies to this marking; this period covers generative systems already on the market before August 2, 2026. According to the same source, for artistic and creative works, the obligation is limited to disclosing the existence of the content in a way that does not spoil the work's enjoyment.

The EU rule is not directly binding in Turkey. Still, if you offer content or systems aimed at the EU market, review their scope with the guide on [the EU AI Act and Turkey](/rehber/ab-yapay-zeka-yasasi-ve-turkiye/). For EU-specific penalty amounts, check the official text; do not rely solely on figures appearing on a technology company's website.

## Practical summary and limits

If you are a victim of a deepfake, act quickly: save the evidence, file a complaint with the platform, apply to the competent authorities and get support from a lawyer. If you are creating or sharing content, obtain the explicit consent of the person concerned, make sure the content is not misleading, and make it a habit to disclose that it was generated by AI. Much of the legal information in Turkey relies on law firms' interpretations, and these interpretations can differ. For penalty ranges, time limits and the correct respondent for applications, consult the official legislation text and seek expert advice.
