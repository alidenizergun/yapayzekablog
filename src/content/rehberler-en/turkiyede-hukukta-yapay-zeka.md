Artificial intelligence is being debated in Turkey's legal field on three fronts: the state's digital initiatives in the judiciary, the legal profession's own ethical framework, and the protection of personal data. This guide summarizes all three with their sources, from a practical perspective. It is not legal advice; for any specific situation, consult a lawyer or data protection expert.

## What the Justice Ministry and UYAP have planned

The Justice Ministry plans to use AI in its call line and case-handling processes. According to [Anadolu Agency](https://www.aa.com.tr/tr/gundem/yargida-dijitallesme-ve-yapay-zeka-donemi-hiz-kazaniyor/4104637), the AI-supported Alo Adalet 135 Call Line will run in pilot provinces for applications concerning delays in cases that have been ongoing for more than five years and in investigations that have been ongoing for more than three years.

Justice Minister Akın Gürlek also told the Plan and Budget Committee of the Turkish Grand National Assembly that the UYAP Artificial Intelligence Decision Support System is being developed with domestic and national resources and will be put into pilot use. The story appeared on [SON TV](https://www.son.tv/yargida-yapay-zeka-devri-hakim-ve-savcilara-yerli-yapay-zeka-destegi-geliyor/) on November 9, 2026. Details of the pilot's scope and operation are limited in the report, so for a firm picture of what the system actually does, official announcements should be awaited.

## Decision support tool: where the line is drawn

According to [Toplumsal](https://www.toplumsal.com.tr/yargida-yapay-zeka-destekli-yeni-donem-basliyor), which relayed the ministry's statements, AI is seen as a decision-support tool for judges and prosecutors, and the final decision remains with judicial officials. In practice, this means the system can offer suggestions and information, but decision-making responsibility is not transferred.

The legal and ethical aspects of integrating AI into the judiciary are still under debate. Responding to Gürlek's remarks, the Turkish Bar Association (TBB) said in [a reply reported by T24](https://t24.com.tr/gundem/turkiye-barolar-birliginden-bakan-gurlekin-yapay-zeka-hakkindaki-aciklamalarina-yanit-devletin-oncelikli-gorevi-yurttaslarin-avukata-erisimini-yayginlastirmaktir,1317323) that the state's primary duty should be to widen citizens' access to lawyers, and that AI should not replace defense.

## TBB's approach: use is not unrestricted, and responsibility cannot be delegated

The TBB [published](https://www.barobirlik.org.tr/Haberler/tbb-avukatlikta-yapay-zeka-kullanimina-iliskin-tavsiye-rehberini-yayimladi-86648) its Recommendation Guide on the Use of AI by Lawyers, prepared by its Information Technology and Technology Law Commission, on August 28, 2026. The guide's starting point is that the debate has shifted from whether AI should be used to the limits within which it should be used. The same emphasis was made at the conclusion panel of the TBB's AI and Legal Profession Workshop, held February 14-15, 2026.

According to [news summaries](https://www.724guncelhaber.com/gundem/turkiye-barolar-birligi-avukatlar-icin-yapay-zeka-kullanimi-tavsiye-rehberi-yayinladi-109472), the guide's core principle is that professional responsibility cannot be delegated to AI at any stage. AI outputs must not be submitted to courts, administrative bodies, clients or third parties without verification from official and reliable sources.

The guide also classifies AI use into four groups:

- free auxiliary use,
- limited and controlled use,
- use subject to special safeguards and, where necessary, approval,
- prohibited use.

According to the TBB announcement, the guide recommends that firms adopt a written AI use policy. It also includes sample clauses for client agreements and control questions for evaluating AI service providers.

## How to get started in your firm

Based on the guide's recommendations, you can set up a simple starting plan:

1. **Classify your uses.** Separate tasks where you use AI only as an aid (for example, proofreading a text you wrote yourself) from those where client data is involved.
2. **Write a written policy.** A [firm-wide AI policy](/rehber/sirket-ici-yapay-zeka-politikasi/) guide can provide a framework for a common rule across the office.
3. **Verify outputs.** If a statute article, case number or doctrinal citation came from AI, check it individually against an official source. We covered the risk of fabricated case law in [a separate guide](/rehber/hukukta-yapay-zeka-uydurma-ictihat-riski/).
4. **Evaluate the tool.** Find out from the contract and privacy policy where the service processes data, whether it stores that data, and whether it uses it for training.

For a general overview of use cases in legal practice, see our [AI for lawyers](/rehber/avukatlar-icin-yapay-zeka/) guide.

## What lawyers need to know under KVKK

The Personal Data Protection Authority (KVKK) published the Implementation Guide on the Protection of Personal Data in Lawyers' Professional Activities, prepared with input from the TBB, on September 22, 2026. [The guide](https://www.turkiyehukuk.org/avukatlarin-mesleki-faaliyetlerinde-kisisel-verilerin-korunmasina-iliskin-uygulama-rehberi-yayimlandi/) covers the use of generative AI tools.

According to [a law firm's summary](https://www.erdem-erdem.com/bilgi-bankasi/avukatlarin-mesleki-faaliyetlerinde-kisisel-verilerin-korunmasina-iliskin-uygulama-rehberi-yayimlandi), the guide stresses that lawyers' duty of confidentiality and their duty of data security do not cancel each other out, and it refers to Article 9 of KVKK, which regulates cross-border transfers of data. This raises an important question in daily practice: could uploading client files to an AI tool running on overseas servers amount to a transfer of personal data? The answer depends on how the tool works and on its contract terms. In unclear cases, get advice from a data protection specialist lawyer.

In daily use, these habits reduce risk:

- Where possible, work with texts from which names, Turkish ID numbers, addresses and similar details have been removed or generalized.
- Prefer corporate solutions secured by contract over personal accounts.
- Keep a record within the firm of which data is processed with which tool.

For general principles on using AI with personal data, see our [personal data protection guide](/rehber/yapay-zeka-kullanirken-kisisel-verileri-koruma/). The TBB guide also says that personal data and client data should not be entered into AI tools without an approved closed system and the necessary legal safeguards. However, the wording in the guide's PDF could not be verified for this article, so it should be read as a general direction rather than a firm rule.

## Contested and open issues

Some information is still incomplete. [News reports](https://kronos42.news/yargi-paketi-yapay-zeka-vurgulu/) say the 13th Judicial Package will include labeling requirements for AI-generated content and measures against deepfakes and fraud. However, this information is circulating with reference to ministry sources, and whether the package has been submitted to parliament could not be verified for this article. Treat it as a proposal on the agenda, not as an enacted regulation.

Similarly, the scope and timing of AI features to be added to UYAP are mostly being followed through statements reported in the press. Until an official directive or regulation is published, it is reasonable to approach the details with caution.

## Quick checklist

- Never submit AI output anywhere without verifying its source.
- Do not enter client data into tools without safeguards.
- Create a written use policy in your firm and share it with your staff.
- Read the TBB and KVKK guides together: one addresses ethics and professional responsibility, the other personal data obligations.
- When in doubt, consult your bar association or a lawyer with expertise in the area.
