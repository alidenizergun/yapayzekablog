Marketing platforms now talk about agents that, when given a goal, carry out several steps on their own, not just assistants that write text. This guide summarizes what AI agents can do today in tasks such as campaign setup, reporting, and content production, what Salesforce, HubSpot, and Adobe have announced, and what to watch for when adding these tools to your team. For the broader picture, see the [AI use cases in marketing](/rehber/pazarlamada-yapay-zeka-kullanim-alanlari/) guide.

## How is an agent different from an assistant?

With a chat assistant, you tell it what to do at every step. An [AI agent](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ajanlarini-guvenle-kullanma/), given a goal and boundaries, plans the intermediate steps itself, connects to tools and data, and moves the work forward. In marketing, this means an instruction like 'set up a campaign for this target audience with this budget' gets split into several tasks.

How much work an agent can do depends on the data it is connected to and the limits you set. That is why deciding which work you will hand over matters more than which agent you choose.

## Campaign setup: the Salesforce example

In its June 3, 2026 [announcement](https://www.salesforce.com/news/stories/agentic-marketing-teams-announcement/), Salesforce introduced the Agentforce Marketing Goals Agent. Marketers enter the goal, audience, budget, and limits; the agent aims to build and run the campaign within those limits and optimize it. Salesforce says the product is currently in pilot.

This detail matters: an agent that runs campaign setup end to end is not yet a product everyone can use, at least in this offering. Ask the vendor for current information on general availability and pricing. Do not accept claims from a single blog post, such as 'it runs without human triggers' or a specific per-use price, without confirmation from Salesforce's own statements.

## HubSpot: ready-made agents tied to CRM data

According to HubSpot's [official AI page](https://www.hubspot.com/products/artificial-intelligence), there are ready-made agents for marketing, sales, and customer service, and you can also build custom agents. The agents use customer data from the CRM. The same page says some agents consume HubSpot Credits, while others are included in the price of the relevant product, so you need to check the cost agent by agent.

One example listed as beta on the page is a feedback agent that finds recurring objections in sales data and passes them to the marketing team. Agent lists and beta labels change often, so check the current status on the official page before investing in an agent. Price figures in third-party reviews are also inconsistent across sources.

## Adobe: workflows at enterprise scale

Adobe's approach is aimed mainly at large-scale enterprise workflows. In its June 22, 2026 [press release](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260622975644/en/Adobe-Accelerates-Agentic-AI-Adoption-Through-New-Agency-and-Technology-Partnerships), the company announced that CX Enterprise Coworker and Adobe Marketing Agent can also be used on platforms such as AWS, Anthropic, Google Cloud, Microsoft, and OpenAI, and that they connect through Model Context Protocol (MCP) servers.

On the Adobe Marketo Engage side, according to [Adobe's explanation](https://business.adobe.com/blog/agentic-ai-redefining-user-experience-marketing-ops), agents handle building, maintaining, and optimizing programs through natural-language commands. The company says this is done with less manual intervention and stronger safeguards. Such features mostly matter for large teams that already use the platform.

## Reporting, briefs, and content: where is it used most today?

According to Basis's [report](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/19/3383456/0/en/daily-ai-use-nearly-doubles-among-marketers-as-measurement-lags.html) dated October 19, 2026, 61.4% of the 184 marketing and advertising professionals surveyed in August 2026 use generative or agent-based AI every day. 51.1% of organizations use agents. The agent use cases are ranked as follows:

- Reporting and analytics: 72.3%
- Campaign brief creation: 56.4%
- Content production: 45.7%

So agents are used most in data-heavy work whose output is easy to verify. Basis presents itself as a vendor of 'autonomous advertising' platforms and the sample is small, so it is better to read these results as a trend indicator rather than a definitive picture.

In the platform announcements we reviewed, we did not see a standout agent focused on content calendars or competitor tracking. If you encounter a product claim in these areas, ask for sample outputs and source citations. For brand consistency on the content side, see the [brand voice guide](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ile-icerik-uretimi-ve-marka-sesi/).

## Human approval and brand safety

According to the same Basis data, 76.6% of marketers think AI content falls short of human-made content. Only 6.5% measure AI return on investment against defined metrics. In addition, all respondents agree that AI carries brand safety and misinformation risks.

A [study](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/alter-agents-and-smartly-release-new-research-showing-99-of-marketers-use-ai-with-human-creativity-and-oversight-key-to-its-future-302920296.html) by Smartly and Alter Agents, dated October 27, 2026, shows a similar picture. In a survey of 250 marketing professionals in the US, 77% accept AI in advertising when a human gives the final approval, and 81% say they would trust a brand more if it openly labels ads produced with AI. Both studies are vendor-sourced and US-focused, so they indicate a direction rather than a firm claim.

In practice, you can do the following:

1. Write separate lists of the work an agent can publish directly and the work that requires human approval.
2. Set upper limits for budget, target audience, and message tone.
3. Have a person review every piece of content before it goes live, especially content with claims, prices, and discounts.
4. Keep a record of the changes the agent makes, and define in advance how to reverse them.

To put this framework in writing across the company, the [company AI policy](/rehber/sirket-ici-yapay-zeka-politikasi/) guide can help.

## Advertising rules in Turkey

An amendment to Turkey's Regulation on Commercial Advertising and Unfair Commercial Practices was published in the Official Gazette No. 33297 on July 1, 2026, and took effect on August 1, 2026. According to [law firm summaries](https://celikhukuk.av.tr/reklamlarda-yapay-zeka-kullanimi-1-agustos-2026-yonetmeligi/), if AI is used in an ad in a way that significantly affects consumers' economic behavior, or if the ad features a digital character that cannot be distinguished from a human, this must be disclosed clearly and in a distinguishable way. [The same amendment](https://foundern.com/2026/07/ticaret-bakanligi-yeni-duzenleme/) also bans profiling children based on personal data and targeted advertising aimed at them.

This information is based on summaries; verify the full text of the provisions and what they mean for your team with your legal counsel. KVKK (Personal Data Protection Law) obligations also apply to agents that process personal data; for details, see the [personalization and KVKK guide](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ile-kisisellestirme-ve-kvkk/). There is no clear information on whether Salesforce Agentforce, HubSpot Breeze, or Adobe agents are available in Turkey, whether they support Turkish, or what their pricing in Turkish lira is; request written confirmation from your sales representative.

## How to start and what to expect

Start small. Pick a task whose output you can easily check, such as reporting or campaign briefs, and run a trial of two to three weeks. Define success upfront: how much time was saved, how many corrections were needed, and what types of errors occurred. The low share of marketers who measure AI return on investment suggests that skipping this step is common.

A realistic expectation is that agents can ease repetitive coordination and preparation work, but strategy, brand decisions, and approval remain yours. Hallucinations, off-brand tone, and targeting errors do not go away. Keep in mind that some announced products are in pilot or beta, and before committing to your team, ask the vendor explicitly about limits, record-keeping, and rollback capabilities.
