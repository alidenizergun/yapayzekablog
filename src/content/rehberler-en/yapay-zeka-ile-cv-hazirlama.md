AI assistants save a lot of time on the two hardest parts of writing a résumé: turning your experience into effective sentences and tailoring the CV to each job posting. But for a good result, you need to supply the information and let the AI organize it.

## 1. Gather your raw information

Start by preparing your own notes: where you worked and when, your responsibilities, the projects you led, measurable results (sales growth, shorter turnaround times, the budget or team you managed), education, certifications and skills. The more concrete the numbers, the stronger the result. The notes can be messy; you'll leave the organizing to the AI.

## 2. Remove personal information

When giving the assistant your information, you don't need to include details such as your name, phone number, address or ID number. You'll add them to the CV yourself at the final stage.

## 3. Turn your experience into achievement statements

**Prompt template:** "Rewrite my job experience notes below as bullet points for a CV. Start each bullet with a strong verb, include a measurable result where possible, and keep each to two lines at most. Do not add any information, figures or skills that are not in the notes."

The last sentence matters: to fill gaps, models can invent figures that look plausible but aren't real.

## 4. Tailor it to the job posting

Give the assistant the posting you're applying to along with your CV: "Based on this job posting, which experiences in my CV should I highlight? Which skills requested in the posting appear to be missing from my CV? Only reorder and rephrase experience I actually have." This lets you shift the focus of your CV for each application.

## 5. Have it draft the summary (profile) section

The 3-4 sentence summary at the top of a CV is the first place a reader looks. Use a prompt such as "Based on my experience, write a 3-sentence professional summary for a [position] role; avoid clichés," get a few options, and edit the best one into your own voice.

## 6. Prepare a cover letter

For the cover letter, provide the posting, your CV and what you know about the company: "Write a warm but professional cover letter of no more than 250 words for this application. Connect why I'm interested in the company's work on [product/goal] with this experience of mine: …" Always verify anything the AI writes about the company.

## 7. Do the final check yourself

Read the CV from start to finish and ask yourself: Can I explain every line with an example in an interview? Are the figures correct? Does the language sound like me? Are there typos or date inconsistencies? You can also ask the assistant for feedback: "Critique this CV as a recruiter would and point out the weak spots."

## What to avoid

- Adding skills, titles or certifications you don't have.
- Sending the same generic CV to every posting.
- Stock phrases found on every CV, like "results-oriented, dynamic, team player."
- Overly complex design templates: applicant tracking systems read simple formats better.
