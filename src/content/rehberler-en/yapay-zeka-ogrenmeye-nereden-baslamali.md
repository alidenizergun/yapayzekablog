AI has become a topic of conversation in every profession, and many people are asking the same question: Where should I start? The answer depends on what you want to do with AI. Below you'll find a roadmap for three different goals. All of them start with the same first step.

## 1. Learn the core concepts

The first step for everyone is knowing what commonly used terms mean: artificial intelligence, machine learning, deep learning, large language model, token, prompt, hallucination, AI agent. [Our AI glossary](/sozluk/) explains these concepts in plain language. This foundation is enough to follow the news and the debates.

## 2. Use the tools every day

Pick one or two AI assistants and use them in your real work: writing emails, summarizing documents, learning a topic, generating ideas. Our guides on [how to use ChatGPT](/rehber/chatgpt-nasil-kullanilir/) and [how to write a good prompt](/rehber/iyi-istem-nasil-yazilir/) are a good starting point. Seeing for yourself what works well and where the tools make mistakes is the most valuable way to learn.

## 3. Choose your goal

### Path A: Informed user
If your aim is to use AI efficiently and safely in daily life and at work: learn prompt-writing techniques, compare different tools, know the privacy and security rules, and build awareness of deepfakes and misinformation.

### Path B: Professional applying AI in their job
If you work in a field such as marketing, finance, human resources, law or operations: identify the repetitive tasks in your own job, build AI workflows for them, learn no-code automation tools, and understand the basic technical concepts (API, RAG, agents, cost) needed to manage AI projects.

### Path C: Developer or data specialist
If you want to build AI applications: learn Python programming, build small projects using AI companies' APIs, and experiment with RAG and agent systems. To go deeper, learn the fundamentals of machine learning, neural networks and the transformer architecture; this requires knowledge of linear algebra and probability.

## 4. Build small projects

Whichever path you choose, apply what you learn in a project: automating a report at your job, building an assistant that answers questions from your own documents, or developing a small web app. Projects make theoretical knowledge stick and give you a concrete example for your résumé.

## 5. Stay current

In AI, models, tools and prices change every few months. Follow reliable news sources, try new tools on your own work, and approach exaggerated claims with skepticism. [YZ Gündemi's weekly roundup](/haftalik/) gathers the week's most important developments on a single page.

## 6. Don't neglect ethics and security

AI's risks, such as bias, privacy, copyright and security, concern every user. Knowing about these issues lets you both protect yourself and make responsible decisions in your work.
