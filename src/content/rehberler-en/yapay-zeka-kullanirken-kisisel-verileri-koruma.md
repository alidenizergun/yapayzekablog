AI assistants are spreading quickly in the workplace, used for everything from writing emails to reviewing contracts. But every piece of information typed into these tools is sent to the servers of the company providing them. This guide explains the basic rules you can apply to protect personal data and company information. It is not legal advice; consult your legal department for your company's specific situation.

## What should you not enter?

Avoid entering the following information into general-purpose AI tools used with a personal account:

- **Identity and contact information:** Turkish national ID numbers, addresses, phone numbers, email lists.
- **Special categories of personal data:** Health information, religion, ethnic origin, biometric data and other data that KVKK (Turkey's Personal Data Protection Law) protects more strictly.
- **Financial information:** Card and account numbers, salary information.
- **Customer and employee data:** Customer lists, CRM exports, personnel files.
- **Trade secrets:** Unpublished financial results, merger and acquisition plans, source code, passwords and API keys.

## Know the tool you use

Even tools from the same company can have different data policies depending on the account type. Find the answers to these questions in the tool's privacy settings and policy:

- Are my conversations stored, and for how long?
- Is what I type used to train the model, and can I turn that off?
- In which country is the data processed?
- Is there stricter protection on an enterprise or team account?

Many tools offer an option to turn off data use for model training on personal accounts; on enterprise accounts, data is generally not used for training by default.

## Clean the data before you enter it

If you need an AI to summarize or edit a text, first remove or replace the personal information. For example, you can use "Customer A" instead of the customer's name and rounded figures instead of real amounts. The task can often be done without this information.

## Recommendations for companies

- **Written policy:** Prepare a short, clear AI usage policy that specifies which tools can be used, for which tasks and with which data.
- **Approved tool list:** Instead of letting employees use random tools with personal accounts, provide enterprise accounts whose data protection terms have been reviewed.
- **Separate solutions for sensitive work:** For work where data must not leave the company, consider open-weight models running on your own servers or enterprise services with a signed data processing agreement.
- **Training:** Explain to employees, with concrete examples, what information must not be shared.
- **KVKK obligations:** Work with your legal department on issues such as transferring personal data abroad, disclosure notices and explicit consent.

## Quick checklist

Before typing a piece of information into an AI tool, ask yourself: Would it be a problem if this appeared in a newspaper? If the answer is yes, either remove the information or use an approved, secure enterprise tool.
