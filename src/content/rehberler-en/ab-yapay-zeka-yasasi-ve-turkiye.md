The EU Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act) is the first comprehensive regulation that classifies AI systems by risk level. Even if your company is based in Turkey, this framework may apply to you if you offer products or services in Europe. The timeline changed in 2026 with the Omnibus amendment, so be cautious with articles that cite the old dates. The summary below is for information only and is not legal advice; consult a lawyer about your specific situation.

## Risk classes: four tiers

The regulation sets up four main tiers: prohibited practices, high-risk systems subject to strict requirements, limited-risk uses that trigger transparency obligations, and general-purpose AI (GPAI) models ([Köksal Avukatlık Ortaklığı](https://koksal.av.tr/mevzuat/eu-ai-act-yapay-zeka-tuzugu/)). The higher the tier, the heavier the obligations.

The key question for your business is which tier your product falls into. That is determined by what the product is used for, not by the underlying technology. The same model could be limited-risk in a customer support chatbot and high-risk in a hiring tool.

## High risk: which systems and which obligations

Annex III covers areas such as hiring, credit, education, critical infrastructure and biometrics. High-risk systems include recruitment and employee evaluation, creditworthiness, assessment in education, critical infrastructure, and biometric identification ([KVKK Rehberi](https://kvkkrehberi.com/blog/eu-ai-act-turk-sirketleri/)). We covered the risks in hiring separately in our guide on AI in recruitment and bias.

What is expected of a high-risk system provider includes: establishing quality management; and meeting requirements for data governance, technical documentation, record-keeping, human oversight, accuracy and cybersecurity resilience ([Köksal Avukatlık Ortaklığı](https://koksal.av.tr/mevzuat/eu-ai-act-yapay-zeka-tuzugu/)). A provider established outside the EU that wants to place its high-risk system on the EU market must also appoint an authorized representative established in the EU ([KVKK Rehberi](https://kvkkrehberi.com/blog/eu-ai-act-turk-sirketleri/)).

The Omnibus text also calls for a written contract between a high-risk system provider and a component supplier. The provider and the third-party supplier should use this contract to define the information, expertise and technical access the supplier must provide ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng)). If you source a model or component from outside, this provision will directly affect your supplier contracts.

## Current timeline

The regulation that postpones the high-risk rules is Digital Omnibus on AI, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744. It was adopted on July 8, 2026 and amends the AI Act (2024/1689) ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng)). Key dates:

- **Annex III (standalone high-risk systems):** the old date was August 2, 2026; compliance will now be required from December 2, 2027 ([i-SCOOP](https://www.i-scoop.eu/eu-ai-act-timeline-digital-omnibus/)).
- **Annex I (systems embedded in products):** August 2, 2028 for systems in products such as machinery, toys and elevators ([Technology Org](https://www.technology.org/2026/10/13/eu-ai-act-deadlines-after-the-digital-omnibus-what-applies-from-december-2026-to-august-2028/)).
- **Public authorities:** August 2, 2030 applies to legacy high-risk systems used by public authorities ([i-SCOOP](https://www.i-scoop.eu/eu-ai-act-timeline-digital-omnibus/)).
- **Synthetic content:** systems placed on the market before August 2, 2026 that generate synthetic content must comply with Article 50(2) by December 2, 2026 ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2026/1744/oj/eng)).

Some Turkish-language sources still give August 2, 2027 for Annex I. That date reflects the situation before the Omnibus; the current date is 2028. Do not build a timeline without checking when a source was published.

## Transparency rules were not postponed

The biggest beneficiaries of the delay are high-risk system providers. Transparency obligations are different. If you operate a [chatbot](/rehber/chatgpt-nasil-kullanilir/) aimed at the EU or produce synthetic media, your duty to inform users under Article 50 is relevant. You can find more on the risks of synthetic images and audio in our [deepfake guide](/rehber/deepfake-nasil-anlasilir/). If you have a consumer-facing product, start your compliance schedule based on these rules, not on the high-risk dates.

## How it affects Turkish companies

The regulation does not look at where the provider is established. The test is whether the system is placed on the EU market or its output is used within the EU ([Gibibyte](https://gibibyte.com.tr/blog/eu-ai-act-agustos-2026-erteleme)). According to an example scenario in one source, an Istanbul company selling a CV-screening tool to European customers would be considered a high-risk system provider. This is an interpretive example, not an official decision. According to the same source, systems that serve only Turkey and whose output is not used in the EU are not directly covered ([Altaysec Akademi](https://akademi.altaysec.com.tr/blog/ab-yapay-zeka-yasasi-ai-act)).

There is also indirect pressure: EU customers may ask you to meet these rules in contracts because of their own compliance obligations ([Altaysec Akademi](https://akademi.altaysec.com.tr/blog/ab-yapay-zeka-yasasi-ai-act)). So even if you are not directly covered, you may receive a checklist of questions as a supplier.

## Penalties

According to secondary sources, fines for prohibited practices can reach 35 million euros or 7% of global annual turnover, whichever is higher. For other obligations the cap is 15 million euros or 3%, and for misleading information 7.5 million euros or 1% ([Regulation-AI.eu](https://www.regulation-ai.eu/en/penalties/)). Verify these amounts against the official text as well, and check the current status of enforcement separately.

## AI regulation efforts in Turkey

The TBMM website hosts a bill page titled [Artificial Intelligence Bill (2/2234)](https://tbmm.gov.tr/Yasama/KanunTeklifi/e21539a0-888a-4500-81be-01904a918c53). Read the scope and status of the bill directly from the source. A comparison published by a law firm covers three bills submitted to the TBMM. Bills at the proposal stage can change, so rather than building a roadmap on the assumption of a Turkish law that does not yet exist, it is more sound to anchor on the EU timeline and monitor developments in Turkey. For the Turkish legal framework, you can also see our guide on AI and law in Turkey.

## Practical steps to prepare

1. **Take inventory.** List the AI systems you develop or use, and whether they reach the EU market or users in the EU.
2. **Determine the risk tier.** Match each system's purpose to the regulation's tiers, and prioritize checks on Annex III areas such as hiring, credit and education.
3. **Review contracts.** Clarify information and access terms with your component or model suppliers.
4. **Implement the transparency rule early.** Set up user-notification flows for chatbots and products that generate synthetic content.
5. **Write an internal policy.** A company AI policy is a starting point you can show both EU customers and auditors.
6. **Get legal support.** Expert advice is essential, especially on the authorized representative requirement and high-risk obligations.

The limits are clear too: the timeline and interpretations may change, and secondary sources can contain errors. Check the official text and the current status before making decisions.
