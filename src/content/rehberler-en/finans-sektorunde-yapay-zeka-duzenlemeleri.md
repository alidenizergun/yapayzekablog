Financial institutions use AI in many areas, from credit assessment to fraud detection, customer service and market surveillance. Regulators are responding to this use at different speeds. This guide summarizes the statements and steps of Turkish institutions (BDDK (Banking Regulation and Supervision Agency), SPK (Capital Markets Board), TCMB (Central Bank of the Republic of Turkey), KVKK (Turkey's Personal Data Protection Authority)), the place of credit scoring in the EU AI Act, and the approach of the UK's Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). US regulators are outside the scope of this guide; for current information, check the official websites of the relevant agencies. This text is not legal advice or investment advice.

## Overview in Turkey

For now, no binding text that directly regulates AI specifically for the financial sector appears to have been published in Turkey. Instead, institutions are laying the groundwork through strategy plans, workshops and their own internal practices. Sanction and supervisory authority over AI in the sector also remains with the relevant regulators. The practical approach for financial institutions is therefore to apply existing legislation to AI use and to monitor new regulations.

For detailed use cases in banking, see the [Bankacılıkta yapay zeka](/rehber/bankacilikta-yapay-zeka/) guide (Artificial intelligence in banking).

## BDDK: strategy plan and workshop

The BDDK's formal basis is its [2025-2028 Strategic Plan](https://www.bddk.org.tr/KurumHakkinda/EkGetir/27?ekId=464). The plan states that procedures and principles for AI use will be determined. In other words, the direction is clear, but the rules have not yet taken shape.

In February 2026, the BDDK and the Credit Registry Bureau (KKB) held an Artificial Intelligence and Cloud Technologies Workshop on February 13-15. [According to ParaMedya](https://www.paramedya.com/devami/140107/bddk-ve-kkbden-finans-sektorune-yapay-zeka-yol-haritasi/), the presentation covered AI committees at banks, algorithmic auditing and draft risk-based regulation. These are agenda items, not rules in force. Sector commentary indicates that model risk management, regular stress testing and validation also came to the fore, but no official BDDK statement in this direction has been reported.

## SPK: AI in market surveillance

The notable development at the SPK is that AI is being discussed as a surveillance tool rather than as a regulated technology. Vice President Cevdet Yılmaz announced that an AI-supported project would be launched within the SPK to detect market manipulation in near real time. Yılmaz also said a new law that would toughen penalties for manipulation would be submitted to parliament. Details appear in [the CNBC-e report](https://www.cnbce.com/borsa/spk-hisseleri-tek-tek-inceliyor-borsada-cezalar-agirlasacak-yapay-zeka-devreye-girecek-h41826).

This carries an indirect message for investment firms: trading behavior may be monitored more closely with AI-supported tools. For those considering AI in investment decisions, there is also a guide on [Yatırım yaparken yapay zeka kullanmak](/rehber/yatirim-yaparken-yapay-zeka-kullanmak/) (Using AI when investing).

## TCMB and KVKK

For now, the central bank appears as a user rather than a regulator. The [TCMB 2025 Annual Report](https://www3.tcmb.gov.tr/yillikrapor/2025/m-2-12/) says that account-name matching for money transfers to institution accounts has been automated with an agent-based AI service. According to the same report, research is underway into detecting unusual transaction patterns in payment data using AI. This information concerns the institution's own internal operations; it does not amount to an AI-specific rule for payment institutions.

On personal data, the KVKK published [an announcement on agentic AI](https://www.kvkk.gov.tr/Icerik/8683/etken-yapay-zeka-agentic-ai) on March 12, 2026. The announcement says that the growing autonomy of systems creates new areas of assessment for the protection of personal data. Any AI application working with customer data cannot be considered independently of KVKK obligations. You can start with the guide [Yapay zeka kullanirken kisisel verileri koruma](/rehber/yapay-zeka-kullanirken-kisisel-verileri-koruma/) (Protecting personal data when using AI).

## Credit scoring under the EU AI Act

Annex III, point 5(b) of the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) classifies AI used for credit assessment or credit scoring of natural persons as high-risk. Risk assessment and pricing in life and health insurance fall under point 5(c). These details appear in [regulatoryai.eu's financial services map](https://www.regulatoryai.eu/ai-financial-services/).

Being classified as high-risk does not mean being banned; systems become subject to additional rules. For the details of these rules and how they bind you, work with your legal advisor. A caution: published guides disagree on the application date. Some give August 2, 2026, while many give December 2, 2027. Verify the date against the EU's official text before you plan.

For the general framework in Turkey, see the guide [AB Yapay Zeka Yasası ve Türkiye](/rehber/ab-yapay-zeka-yasasi-ve-turkiye/) (The EU AI Act and Turkey).

## UK: the FCA's approach

In the UK, the Financial Conduct Authority (FCA) is not drafting a separate AI rulebook for the financial sector. [According to Regulatory Counsel](https://regulatorycounsel.co.uk/insights/fca-ai-governance-financial-services-2026), the regulator says it will rely on existing frameworks and will continue to review its approach.

On October 19, 2026, the FCA published a page titled "AI in financial services: good practice and areas for improvement". [According to the Global Financial Regulation Blog](https://www.globalfinregblog.com/2026/10/fca-issues-guidance-for-firms-on-their-use-of-ai/), the guidance focuses on the Consumer Duty, governance and accountability, and operational resilience. The message can be summarized this way: do not expect a new rulebook; assume that existing obligations apply to your use of AI as well.

## Where to start within your organization

Even though published rules are limited, there is no need to wait to prepare. You could try the following:

- **Build an inventory.** List which processes use AI, what data they work with, and who is responsible.
- **Classify risk.** Flag separately the uses that directly affect customers, such as credit decisions; the EU's high-risk category can serve as a reference.
- **Test explainability and bias.** Make sure you can explain why a decision was made and that no particular groups receive unfair outcomes.
- **Review personal data.** Check data flows and the providers you use against KVKK requirements.
- **Do not outsource responsibility.** Manage your relationship with the provider through contracts and oversight.
- **Write an internal policy.** The guide on [Şirket içi yapay zeka politikası](/rehber/sirket-ici-yapay-zeka-politikasi/) (Company AI policy) offers a framework for this.

## Limits and cautions

Information in this area changes quickly. Drafts, workshop presentations and commentary are not binding rules. When a regulation is mentioned, check the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete), the agency's official announcement or the EU's published text. Do not rely solely on provisions cited in secondary sources. If your organization needs a binding assessment, consult a specialist legal or compliance advisor. You can find other guides and news on the sector in the [Finansta yapay zeka](/sektor/finans/) (AI in finance) section.
