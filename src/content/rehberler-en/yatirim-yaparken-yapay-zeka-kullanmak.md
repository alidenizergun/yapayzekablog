AI chatbots can make it easier to read lengthy financial documents, explain unfamiliar terms and organize news flows. The same tools are also used in fraud, through claims such as "AI tells you which stock to buy." This guide separates the two sides: how you can use AI as a helper in investment research, and which promises you should view with suspicion. The guide does not give investment advice; for your decisions, you need to consult authorized institutions and experts.

## What is AI useful for in investing?

AI is valuable as a reading and learning aid, not as a decision-maker. Its sensible uses fall into three areas: summarizing company reports and financial statements, tracking news flow and learning financial concepts. The common principle across all three is this: the tool saves you time digesting information, but verifying its accuracy is your job.

## Summarizing reports and financial statements

The SPK's [Investing Guide: What Should I Watch Out For](https://spk.gov.tr/data/61e34f9a1b41c61270320792/Yat%C4%B1r%C4%B1m%20Yaparken%20Nelere%20Dikkat%20Etmeliyim.pdf) recommends reading publicly available financial statements and special disclosures. Because these documents are long and technical, AI can be genuinely useful here. A practical method looks like this:

- Obtain the document from the company's own official published source. Do not use a summary or screenshot that someone else has shared.
- Upload the document to the tool and ask for a section-by-section summary. For details, see the [guide to summarizing long documents](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ile-pdf-ve-uzun-belge-ozetleme/).
- In your prompt, ask it to indicate which section of the document each figure comes from, and to say so when it is unsure. For help writing prompts, see the [good prompt guide](/rehber/iyi-istem-nasil-yazilir/).
- Compare every important figure in the summary with its counterpart in the original document.

Do not skip the last step. Chatbots can generate fabricated information (hallucinations). A summary that misstates an amount or presents an inference not found in the document as fact could steer your decision in the wrong direction.

## News tracking and learning concepts

For news tracking, AI can group many headlines and texts for you, and you can ask why a given news item matters. However, a summary is not considered reliable without seeing the news source and date. Open and read the original article the summary is based on.

Learning concepts is the lowest-risk use in this field. For questions such as "What is the difference between a balance sheet and an income statement?" or "What does a special disclosure mean?" you can request clear explanations with examples. Think of the answer as a study note: it helps you grasp the basic logic, but it does not replace an institution's official definition. If a definition matters, check the text of an authoritative body such as the SPK.

## Risks of "AI stock pick" claims

Asking a chatbot "which stock should I buy" is not the same as getting investment advisory from an institution with official authorization. The SPK's guide recommends considering only advice from SPK-authorized institutions and checking the institution's authorization certificate. Koç Holding's [public notice](https://www.koc.com.tr/onemli-uyari) also states that investment advice given on social media or alternative platforms outside SPK-authorized institutions may be treated as market fraud or abuse of trust.

AI also has its own limits. A model does not bear the consequences of your investment decision, does not know your financial situation or risk tolerance, and can produce incorrect or fabricated information. For this reason, a model saying "this stock will rise" is not even a forecast; do not use it as a basis for your investment decisions.

## Fake AI investment platforms

Fraudsters use AI both as an attractive label and as a tool for producing content. In an investigation coordinated by the Mardin Chief Public Prosecutor's Office, suspects who used AI-generated images of state figures to promote investment offers involving "oil, the stock market, Baykar" were caught in an operation across 11 provinces, and [16 people were detained](https://besniekspres.com/asayis/95-milyon-liralik-yapay-zeka-dolandiriciligina-operasyon-16-gozalti/). The same report says that bank transactions of the suspects between September 1, 2025 and January 1, 2026, covering a four-month period, showed approximately 95 million lira in transaction activity. This figure is four-month transaction volume and should not be read as the total amount of victim losses.

A separate article focusing on investment and payment traps is available under [AI-driven financial fraud](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ile-finansal-dolandiricilik/).

## SPK warnings and unlicensed advisory

The SPK has reportedly warned investors against referrals from social media, closed messaging groups and fake websites that promise ["guaranteed returns" or "certain profit"](https://www.gazetebirlik.com/ekonomi/spk-sahte-yatirim-danismanlarina-karsi-vatandaslari-uyardi/1121231). The same report notes that under Article 109 of the Capital Markets Law (SPKn 109), people who provide investment advisory or collect funds without SPK permission face prison sentences of 2 to 5 years.

One detail on authorization matters: according to a [Döviz.com article](https://www.doviz.com/makale/dogru-yatirim-danismani-nasil-secilir/154), SPK authorization certificates are issued to institutions, not to individuals. Therefore, a person or account claiming to be a "licensed AI advisor" should raise suspicion. This information comes from a financial media article; verify the legislation itself and the institution's authorization through the SPK.

The state itself also plans to use AI in market oversight. According to a statement by Vice President Cevdet Yılmaz, the SPK is [reviewing stocks suspected of manipulation one by one](https://www.nefes.com.tr/spk-manipulasyon-suphesi-bulunan-hisseleri-tek-tek-inceliyor-cezalar-agirlasacak-yapay-zeka-devre-164682); within this scope, 123 people were arrested and judicial control orders were issued against 279 people. The same statement reportedly said an [AI-supported initiative will be launched within the SPK](https://www.cnbce.com/borsa/spk-hisseleri-tek-tek-inceliyor-borsada-cezalar-agirlasacak-yapay-zeka-devreye-girecek-h41826) aimed at detecting manipulation faster and in real time. For investors, this is not a guarantee; it is only information that oversight is being strengthened with AI.

## Be skeptical of the "AI-powered" label

In the United States, the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) announced settlements with two investment advisers on March 18, 2024, over [misleading statements about their use of AI](https://corpgov.law.harvard.edu/2024/04/09/sec-fines-two-investment-advisers-for-ai-washing/). These were the first cases in what the SEC calls "AI washing" (presenting AI use as different from what it actually is). According to a law firm's article, the two advisers paid a combined [$400,000 in penalties](https://www.daeryunlaw.com/us/practices/detail/ai-washing); the official SEC text should be verified separately.

The lesson is simple: writing "AI" in a product's marketing does not prove that the product actually uses AI or performs well. Your job is to question the institution's authorization and documents, not the claim.

## Checklist for a safe start

- Use AI for reading, summarizing and learning; do not use it for "buy" or "sell" decisions.
- Verify every important figure against its counterpart in the official document.
- Check the SPK authorization of any person or platform giving investment advice.
- Do not trust guaranteed returns, certain profits, or ads featuring celebrities or state figures.
- Stay away from "signals" and referrals in closed messaging groups.
- If the decision matters, consult an SPK-authorized institution or a licensed professional.
