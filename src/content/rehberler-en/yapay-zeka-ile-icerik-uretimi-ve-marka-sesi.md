AI is one of the easiest ways to speed up a content calendar. But speed alone is not a value: when everyone uses the same tools, everyone's text starts to sound alike, and the first thing to disappear is what sets your brand apart, your voice. This guide lays out practical steps for protecting voice, accuracy and trust when you use AI to create content. For general use cases, you may also want to read the [AI use cases in marketing](/rehber/pazarlamada-yapay-zeka-kullanim-alanlari/) guide.

## Google's stance on AI content

The first question is usually this: will Google push AI-generated content down in search results? According to the sources we have, Google's guidance was updated on October 1, 2026, and says AI can be quite useful for topic research and for giving structure to original content ([Search Atlas's roundup](https://www.searchmaxxed.com/research/scaled-content-abuse)). Another summary says Google does not penalize AI as such; its policy looks at how and why content is produced, not at the tool used ([Crawlspider](https://www.crawlspider.com/google-ai-content-policy)).

The risky area is the "scaled content abuse" policy, announced on March 5, 2024 ([ClearPost](https://clearpostplugin.com/googles-scaled-content-abuse-policy-explained/)). In practice, using AI as part of your writing process is not a problem. But bulk-publishing large numbers of pages that add no value for readers comes close to the behavior the policy targets. These summaries rely on secondary sources, so read Google's own help pages before making any critical decisions.

## Creating a brand voice guide

Telling an AI to "write in our brand's voice" is not enough; the model doesn't know your voice, so you have to define it. A good voice guide is short and actionable:

- **Tone:** Formal or friendly? Do you address readers as "you" in a formal or informal way?
- **Word choices:** Phrases you use often and phrases you never use, overblown adjectives, and Turkish equivalents for foreign terms.
- **Sentence structure:** Short sentences, or longer explanatory paragraphs?
- **Examples:** Two or three texts your brand truly likes, plus one example you'd label "don't do this."

Try to keep the guide to one page. Long, abstract principles ("honest, innovative, human-centered") tell a model very little; concrete examples work far better. When drafting the guide, review your best-performing past texts and write down what they have in common.

## Building a prompt library

Once the voice guide is ready, don't paste it in by hand every time. Build a shared prompt library for your team. It can consist of one prompt template per content type: blog post, email newsletter, product description, social media post. Each template should include:

- The target audience and the purpose of the content
- The relevant section of the voice guide and a sample text
- What information to use and what to avoid (e.g., rely only on the sources you provide)
- The output format and length

Keep the templates in a shared document, record who updated them and when, and fix the prompt whenever outputs fall short. For the basics of prompt writing, the guide [how to write a good prompt](/rehber/iyi-istem-nasil-yazilir/) is a good starting point. The library should be considered together with an internal AI policy that defines who can use which tools for what.

## The role of human editing

However good the prompt, a draft is a draft. An editor's job is not only to catch typos. Check the following:

- **Accuracy:** Large language models can produce made-up information (hallucination). Verify figures, names, dates and quotes against their original sources.
- **Voice:** Does the text match your guide, or is it a generic statement anyone could have written?
- **Originality:** Is there anything in the text that only you could know? A customer story, product experience, your own data or an expert's view are contributions AI cannot provide.
- **Usefulness:** After reading, does the reader learn something or gain a capability?

Don't leave human input until the end; put it at the start too. Have a person define the topic, angle and original material to be used, let AI write the draft, and have a person make the final call. Rather than trying to work out whether a text was written by AI, make sure it adds something real for the reader. The guide [how to tell if a text was written with AI](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ile-yazilmis-metin-nasil-anlasilir/) can also help with this.

## Copyright and originality risks

Copyright law in this area is still not settled, and rules differ from country to country. There are three general risk areas:

- **Ownership uncertainty:** Whether an output produced entirely by AI is legally protected, and who owns it, can vary by country. For your brand's critical assets (slogans, campaign copy, visual identity), document the human contribution.
- **Similarity risk:** A model may produce output that closely resembles existing texts or brands. Check your wording before publishing.
- **Tool terms:** Read the commercial use and content ownership terms of the tools you use.

This is not legal advice. For content that is important or risky for your brand, consult a copyright lawyer. Do not enter customer information or confidential data into prompts.

## Labeling AI content

Labeling is both an ethical issue and an increasingly legal one. What must be labeled, and how, depends on the country, the platform and the type of content, especially visuals and audio that imitate a real person. So first check the current rules in your target markets and the publishing platforms' policies.

Even when it isn't mandatory, transparency often builds trust. You can set an internal benchmark: visuals where AI plays a clear role, synthetic voices and content that evokes a real person should always be disclosed. For minor language corrections or draft support, agree on a common line as a team and write it into your company policy.

## Where to start?

Start small rather than planning a major transformation: choose one content type, write a one-page voice guide, prepare a prompt template specific to that type, and review the outputs with an editor over a few weeks. Note which corrections keep recurring and move them into the prompt. If you're also interested in visibility in search engines and AI assistants, the guide [AI search and brands](/rehber/yapay-zeka-aramalari-ve-markalar-geo/) may be your next step.

Finally, let the benchmark be value, not speed: every text you produce should deserve to carry your brand's name.
