You do not need a large budget or a data science team to start with AI, but starting without a plan is expensive too. For small and mid-sized manufacturers, the most sound approach is to pick one problem, gather the data you already have, run a small pilot and measure the result. This guide offers a practical framework covering first project selection, data preparation, costs, government support and supplier selection. Support information is current as of November 20, 2026; conditions may change, so check official pages before applying.

## Choose the problem first, then the tool

Starting with a technology rather than a problem is one of the most common traps for SMEs. First, write down the issue that constrains you most in the workshop or office: unexpected machine stoppages, the defect rate, excess or shortage of stock, or the time it takes to prepare quotes.

Common starting areas for manufacturers include:

- **Maintenance:** Catching failure signals from machine data. For details, see the [predictive maintenance guide](/rehber/kestirimci-bakim-nedir/).
- **Quality control:** Using camera images to sort out defective parts. You can read the [machine vision quality control guide](/rehber/goruntu-isleme-ile-kalite-kontrol/).
- **Procurement and inventory:** Planning from demand and order data; this topic is covered in the [supply chain and logistics guide](/rehber/tedarik-zinciri-ve-lojistikte-yapay-zeka/).
- **Office work:** Using general-purpose chatbots for daily tasks such as correspondence, translation and report summaries.

A good first project has three traits: its result can be measured in numbers, it involves a single team, and if it fails it does not disrupt production. Do not try to solve everything at once.

## Data preparation: often the real work

AI models, in areas like maintenance and quality, rely on machine learning methods that learn patterns from historical data. The quality of the data directly determines the result. Before you start, answer these questions:

1. **Is there data?** Machine sensors, ERP records, quality forms, maintenance logs and order history are already stored somewhere in most businesses.
2. **Is the data usable?** Paper forms, Excel files kept with inconsistent naming and missing records need to be organized first.
3. **Is there labeling?** If you did not record why a part was defective or when a failure occurred, the model cannot know what it is supposed to learn.
4. **Who owns the data?** Before uploading files that contain customer drawings, designs and employee information to an external tool, check privacy and contract terms. A [company AI policy guide](/rehber/sirket-ici-yapay-zeka-politikasi/) can help you draft a short set of rules on this.

At this stage, the most valuable step is to keep regular, consistent records for several weeks. For a business whose data is not in good order, the first project may be a data collection system rather than a direct AI solution. That is not a loss; every later project will benefit from it.

## Calculate costs and payback upfront

The cost of an AI project is not limited to software or subscription fees. Consider these items together:

- License, cloud or AI infrastructure (data center) services,
- Sensor, camera and connectivity hardware,
- Data preparation and integration work,
- Staff training and adapting the work to the new process,
- Ongoing model monitoring and updates.

Define the payback before you start the project. For example, set a metric such as a reduction in downtime, a drop in scrap rate or a shorter quote preparation time, and record its value before the pilot. Without a starting baseline, you cannot tell whether there was an improvement. Do not apply general productivity and savings figures circulating in sources to your own business; your lines, products and data are different. The pilot result is the only reliable basis for this calculation.

## KOSGEB AI Credit Program (Small and Medium Enterprises Development Organization)

According to [KOSGEB's official announcement](https://www.kosgeb.gov.tr/site/tr/genel/detay/9427/kosgebden-teknoloji-girisimlerine-yapay-zek-kredi-programi), applications are accepted between July 9 and December 31, 2026, and credit amounts range from 500,000 lira to 5 million lira. Applications are made through the KOSGEB SME Information System, and the business must have an active record in the KOSGEB database. [According to Bigpara](https://bigpara.hurriyet.com.tr/haberler/ekonomi-haberleri/yapay-zeka-girisimlerine-yeni-destek_ID102207130/), repayment starts after a 12-month grace period from the outset and is made in 12 equal installments.

Eligible expenses include AI data center services as well as AI tools and platform services. According to Alomaliye, these services are sourced from data centers that have signed protocols with KOSGEB.

Eligibility is the key point to watch. [The announcement from İZTO](https://izto.org.tr/en/duyurular/general-announcements/kosgeb-yapay-zeka-kredi-programi) states that businesses holding a Technology Startup Badge can apply. Since the program appears limited to technology startups, if you are a traditional manufacturer, ask KOSGEB directly whether you can apply for this credit. Verify the application conditions only from the official page.

## TÜBİTAK (Turkey's Scientific and Technological Research Council) and other support

According to [TÜBİTAK's official announcement](https://tubitak.gov.tr/en/funds/funds/industrial/national-support-programs/call-industrial-rd-projects-grant-program-2026-call-2-has-been-opened), the 1501 Industrial R&D Support Program opens two calls a year, and the closing date for the 2026/2 call was October 26, 2026. That means applications are not possible right now; if some third-party guides still say the call is open, that information is outdated. The timing of the next call has not been officially confirmed, so follow TÜBİTAK announcements. [According to the call text](https://tubitak.gov.tr/sites/default/files/2026-07/1501_Sanayi_Ar-Ge_Destek_Programi_2026-2_Proje_Cagri_Metni_13.07.2026_v2.pdf), the TÜBİTAK contribution per project is capped at 20 million lira. 1501 requires a genuine R&D project; buying a ready-made AI tool does not qualify. Therefore, when preparing for the next call, put in writing which technical uncertainty your pilot project is designed to resolve.

In Türkiye, the Ministry of Industry and Technology is also announcing new steps in AI. According to Pekin, the Türkiye Artificial Intelligence Action Plan (2026-2030) was published in the Official Gazette on August 18, 2026, and its goals include 1 gigawatt of installed AI and data center capacity and at least $10 billion in private sector investment. Conditions for programs reported as planned, such as the SME AI voucher and the GPU for Everyone Program, are not yet clear in official sources; do not tie your investment plans to them until an announcement is made.

For SMEs filing a patent application for the first time, [according to Minister Kacır's statement](https://www.haberler.com/ekonomi/sanayi-ve-teknoloji-bakani-kacir-kobi-lere-ilk-20383337-haberi/), a 75% discount applies to application, research and examination report fees. For G06N-class AI applications, there is a 50% discount on the urgent research report fee. If you are developing your own algorithm or method, this item may be useful.

## Points to consider when choosing a consultant or supplier

There are many consultants and solution providers in AI. When choosing one, look at these points:

- **Ask for references in your sector.** General AI experience and manufacturing floor experience are not the same. If possible, ask to see a project at a business of similar scale.
- **Write the success criteria into the contract.** It should be clear from the start what the pilot will prove, with which data and by which measure.
- **Clarify data and intellectual property.** Where will your data be stored, can the supplier use it to develop its own model, and what happens when the contract ends?
- **Personal data and privacy.** If employee or customer data is processed, discuss your obligations under KVKK (Turkey's Personal Data Protection Authority) with the supplier.
- **Limit dependency.** Make sure you can export your data and continue the solution with another provider.
- **Be skeptical of big promises.** Be wary of providers who guarantee exact savings figures or quote without seeing your data.

Also assign an internal owner. If the people who will use the outside solution on the ground are not involved in the process, even the best model will go unused.

## Risks and limits

AI is not the answer to every problem. If data is insufficient, results will be unreliable, the model may issue false alarms, and employees who do not trust the system may quietly abandon the project. Do not leave production floor decisions entirely to a model; keep human oversight, especially on issues affecting occupational safety and product quality. When working with general-purpose chatbots, take care not to share sensitive content such as drawings, price lists and customer information. For a broader view, you can explore the [AI in industry in Türkiye guide](/rehber/turkiyede-sanayide-yapay-zeka/).

In short: start with a single problem, organize your data first, make the payback measurable in advance, verify support from official sources and write the supplier contract clearly. If the pilot result is positive, scale up; if not, learn why and move on to the next attempt.
