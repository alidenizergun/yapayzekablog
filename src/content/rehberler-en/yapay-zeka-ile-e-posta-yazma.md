Writing emails takes up a significant part of the workday. AI assistants can help you find the right tone, especially in difficult or sensitive emails, and turn scattered thoughts into a well-organized message.

## 1. Provide context

Say who you are writing to (a customer, manager, supplier or teammate), what your relationship is and what the email is for. Instead of "Write an email to a customer," say "Write an email to a corporate client we have worked with for three years, informing them that we will delay the project delivery by one week."

## 2. State the main message and the outcome you want

Make clear what the email asks of the reader: getting approval, scheduling a meeting, requesting information or turning down a request. The assistant will structure the text around that goal. It is enough to jot down the points you want to make as rough bullet points.

## 3. Set the tone and length

Tone descriptions such as "formal but warm," "short and clear" or "apologetic but not taking too much responsibility" directly change the result. Set a limit on length, such as "no more than 5 sentences"; shorter texts get read more often in work emails.

## 4. Ask for a subject line

The subject line often decides whether an email gets opened. Ask for options by saying, "Suggest 3 different subject lines for this email, no longer than 8 words."

## 5. Edit the draft

Read the assistant's draft and adapt it to your own style. Cut stock openers like "I hope this email finds you well" and unnecessarily long closings. Be sure to check dates, figures and names.

## Ready-made prompt templates

- **Follow-up email:** "I haven't received a reply to the proposal I sent two weeks ago. Write a 4-sentence follow-up email that serves as a gentle reminder without being pushy."
- **Polite rejection:** "We are turning down a supplier's offer because of price, but we want to work with them in the future. Write a short email that leaves the door open."
- **Apology:** "Write an email apologizing to a customer for a late order delivery, briefly explaining the reason and proposing a solution."
- **Request:** "I want to ask my manager to approve a budget for a new software tool. Write a persuasive email summarizing its benefits and cost."
- **Meeting summary:** "From these meeting notes, prepare a summary email to send to attendees that lists the decisions and who is responsible: …"

## Things to watch out for

Customer details, contract terms and financial data that commonly appear in emails may be confidential. Remove this information from your prompt or replace it with placeholders ([Customer name], [amount]), and fill in the final version yourself.
