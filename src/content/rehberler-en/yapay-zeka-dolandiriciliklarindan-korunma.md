Fraud is nothing new, but AI has strengthened scammers' hand. Phone calls that mimic the voice of a loved one, investment ads built on fake celebrity videos, and tailor-made phishing emails with no typos are now possible. The good news: basic precautions are still very effective.

## Common methods

### Emergency calls using voice cloning
Scammers imitate a relative's voice using audio samples taken from social media videos or a phone call. They use panic-inducing scenarios such as "I had an accident," "I'm in custody" or "I need money urgently" to demand a quick money transfer.

### Deepfake investment ads
AI-generated videos of well-known businesspeople, artists or politicians are used to promote fake investment opportunities such as "guaranteed returns" or an "AI-powered crypto bot."

### Personalized phishing
AI can use information from your social media to write convincing emails and messages tailored to you, free of language errors. They appear to come from your bank, a courier company, a government agency or your workplace.

### Fake executives and business emails
The voice or image of a company executive is imitated to ask accounting or finance staff to make an urgent payment.

### Fake AI apps
Fake apps and browser extensions that use the names of popular AI assistants aim to steal personal or payment information.

## How to protect yourself

1. **Verify through another channel:** Hang up on or close any call or message that asks for urgent money or information, then call the person back on a number you already know.
2. **Set a safe word:** Agree on a word or question within your family that only you know, and ask for it during emergency calls.
3. **Be skeptical of urgency:** Phrases like "right now," "don't tell anyone" and "last chance" are the clearest signs of a scam.
4. **Use official channels:** Instead of clicking links, go to the organization's official website or app yourself.
5. **Download apps from official stores:** Check the developer name and reviews.
6. **Limit what you share on social media:** Long, publicly available audio and video recordings can serve as raw material for voice cloning.
7. **Turn on two-factor authentication:** Add an extra layer of security to your accounts.

## For companies

- For any change to payment or bank details, require approval from a second person and phone verification, no matter who the request comes from.
- Train employees with examples of fake audio and video.
- Set up a predefined verification process for executives' urgent and confidential payment requests.
