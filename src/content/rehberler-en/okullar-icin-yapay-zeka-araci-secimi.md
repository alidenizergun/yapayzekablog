AI chat tools are rapidly entering schools, but choosing one means more than comparing feature lists. Who will use it, which age groups will have access, where student data is stored and for how long, and the total cost are the real deciding factors. This guide covers the education offerings from Gemini, ChatGPT Edu and Claude, along with their age limits, data protection and the questions to ask before buying. Most sources are US-based, so confirm in writing with the vendor whether a product is available to schools in Turkey and what it costs. You can read school-wide rules in our [school AI policy guide](/rehber/okullarda-yapay-zeka-politikasi/).

## Plans and who they are for

**Gemini.** According to Education Week, Google made Gemini features in [Google Classroom available to K-12 students regardless of age starting August 10, 2026](https://www.edweek.org/technology/schools-caught-flat-footed-after-google-makes-gemini-chatbot-available-to-all-students/2026/09). These features were previously limited to users aged 18 and older. The article says schools were caught off guard by the change. The feature only applies if a school administrator has already enabled Gemini; if it was turned off, it stays off. So the first step is to check Gemini's status in the admin console.

**ChatGPT Edu.** In the sources we reviewed, this plan appears mainly on university IT pages. The [University of Washington](https://it.washu.edu/openai-chatgpt-edu/) and University of South Carolina pages state that data entered into ChatGPT Edu is not used to train models. This is a claim you should also verify in your own contract before making any school-level decision.

**Claude.** Anthropic's [Claude for Teachers](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-for-teachers) offer was announced on July 14, 2026, as free access for verified US K-12 teachers. A separate offer for schools and districts opened on August 28, 2026. The program is for individual educators, not students. [Claude for Education](https://www.anthropic.com/news/introducing-claude-for-education) was introduced as an institutional campus plan and is designed primarily for higher education. Campus-wide agreements have been announced with institutions including Northeastern, LSE and Champlain College.

In short, the three products map roughly to three use cases: a chat feature built into a classroom tool, an institutional license, and an individual teacher program. Define your scenario first.

## Age limits and school practices

The age question has not been clearly resolved. Education Week notes that after Gemini was opened to all K-12 students, the question of age appropriateness remained open. Just because a vendor technically allows access does not mean it is pedagogically appropriate.

Many US districts are responding with caution. [Techpolicy.press](https://www.techpolicy.press/in-the-us-caution-rules-on-ai-ahead-of-k12-school-year/) reports that many districts have chosen cautious approaches rather than system-wide rules for the 2026-27 school year. According to one district guideline cited in the article, students under 14 may use only supervised, pre-approved tools, while older students may access approved tools with parental consent and teacher permission. The same article reports that Chicago's approved tool list does not include ChatGPT or Claude, and that Katy ISD has banned generative AI for students in grades K-6.

The lesson: write separate rules for each age band and maintain an approved tool list. Our [AI guide for parents](/rehber/veliler-icin-yapay-zeka-rehberi/) can help you keep families informed.

## Student data and privacy

The most critical security question is whether student information will enter the tool and, if so, under what conditions it will be processed.

Anthropic says Claude for Teachers is configured to handle student records in line with FERPA (the US Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act). However, using identifiable student information requires authorization from the school or district, and that authorization can be granted through the school and district offering. The company also says that student data examples are replaced with anonymized data.

With ChatGPT Edu, the picture varies by institution. [Cumberland University](https://www.ucumberlands.edu/student-life/student-services/information-technology-services/chatgpt-edu) states that the tool is approved only for publicly available data, cannot be used with FERPA or other regulated data, and gives a data retention period of 180 days. WashU says it retains chat history for 18 months. Retention periods and approval scope can differ for the same product, so do not copy another school's approval to your own institution. Read your own contract.

For Gemini, these sources do not include an official data processing document. Request Google's data processing terms in writing and read the text yourself. The general rule is the same for every tool: enter only the minimum necessary data, anonymize it where possible, and do not enter student names, ID numbers, grades or photos into general-purpose tools. For more details, see our [personal data protection guide](/rehber/yapay-zeka-kullanirken-kisisel-verileri-koruma/).

## MEB and KVKK framework in Turkey

MEB (Ministry of National Education) published the [Ethics Guide for AI Applications in Education](https://www.meb.gov.tr/egitimde-yapay-zeka-uygulamalari-etik-kilavuzu-yayimlandi/haber/39496/tr) on January 10, 2026. YAZEK (Artificial Intelligence Applications Ethics Declaration System), developed to ensure compliance with ethical principles, opened for use on February 2, 2026.

According to the [YAZEK page](https://yazek.meb.gov.tr/), an ethics declaration form is required if student data is processed. This covers AI analysis of homework, essays, audio recordings or student grades. The declaration depends not on the platform itself but on whether the teacher is running an AI-powered application on that platform. The page also states that personal and educational data of students and teachers must be protected in full compliance with KVKK (Turkey's Personal Data Protection Authority) and related regulations.

When buying, consider these two areas together: the vendor's data terms and your own KVKK obligations. For questions about cross-border data transfers, retention periods and who the data controller is, the [KVKK and AI tools guide](/rehber/kvkk-ve-yapay-zeka-araclari/) is a good starting point.

## Cost: free is not enough

According to Anthropic's official page, the free Claude for Teachers program was extended to July 30, 2028, in an update dated November 13, 2026. However, the program is for verified US K-12 teachers and for individual use. Moving a school to managed use that processes student data requires a separate school and district offering. Free individual access and a school contract are not the same thing.

For Gemini and ChatGPT Edu, these sources do not include a verified official price page. Get pricing from the vendor in writing and ask about these items separately:

- License cost per user or per institution, and which users are covered
- Whether the price applies for the first year or throughout the contract
- Whether the admin console, usage reports and support services are included
- Time and budget needed for teacher training and the compliance process
- The program end date and transition terms afterward

## Questions to ask before buying

Before moving a tool to the proposal stage, get written answers to these questions:

1. Which age groups can use the tool, and can settings be configured separately by age band?
2. Can an administrator turn the tool on or off at the classroom, teacher or school level?
3. Are student inputs used for model training, and is that stated in the contract?
4. How long is data retained, and how are deletion requests handled?
5. Is a separate privacy agreement offered for student data?
6. How are data processing location and cross-border transfer conditions addressed under KVKK?
7. Is the product usable in Turkey, what level of Turkish language support does it offer, and in what currency is it priced?
8. Will a YAZEK declaration be required for use, and who will file it?
9. When the contract ends, how are data returned or destroyed?

If you cannot get a clear answer to one of these questions, do not roll out that product widely.

## Start with a small pilot

Rather than opening the tool to the entire school at once, start with a few volunteer teachers, a specific grade level and a limited purpose. For practical guides for teachers, you can recommend our [AI for teachers](/rehber/ogretmenler-icin-yapay-zeka/) page, and follow the [AI in education](/sektor/egitim/) section for broader industry developments. At the end of the pilot, record what worked, which hallucination (fabricated information) examples appeared, and where the student data rule broke down. Base your decision on those records, not on the vendor's launch announcement.
