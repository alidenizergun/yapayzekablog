AI chatbots can be both a powerful study partner and a shortcut that easily becomes a crutch for students preparing for exams. The difference lies in how you use the tool. This guide covers practical methods for concept explanations, problem-solving, study planning and mock exam analysis, followed by the error risks, research on learning effects and exam rules. For general guidance on using these tools, you can also read our [AI for students](/rehber/ogrenciler-icin-yapay-zeka/) guide.

## Decide what you will use AI for

The common thread in useful uses is this: the tool supports your thinking rather than replacing it. Four core uses stand out:

- Having a concept explained in a different way when you don't understand it
- Having the logic of a problem you solved explained and finding your error
- Creating a study plan based on your exam date
- Analyzing your practice exam results by topic

None of these requires you to say "just tell me the answer." As you'll see below, asking directly for the answer can weaken learning.

## When asking for explanations, state your level

The biggest problem with concept explanations is that the answer is either too superficial or too advanced. You can solve this in your prompt. Tell it your exam type, the topic and your current level, for example: "Explain exponents at a 10th-grade level with a simple example, then ask me three questions."

A few habits that work:

- Ask it to quiz you at the end of the explanation. Reading an explanation and saying "I get it" is different from being able to answer a question on it.
- Summarize the topic in your own words and ask the model to point out gaps.
- If an explanation seems wrong or contradictory, go back to your textbook or teacher.

The [how to write a good prompt](/rehber/iyi-istem-nasil-yazilir/) guide can help you learn more about writing prompts.

## Try the solution yourself first, then ask for an explanation

The safest sequence for solving problems is this: try it yourself first, identify where you get stuck, then ask the AI for a hint only for that step. Writing out your solution and saying "where is my mistake? Guide me without giving the solution" is far more productive than reading a ready-made answer.

OpenAI has built this approach into its product. According to the company's announcement, the study mode it added to ChatGPT in July 2025 [](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-study-mode/) works step by step, asks guiding questions and encourages thinking instead of giving direct answers. It is recommended for test prep and homework help. If the tool you use doesn't have such a mode, you can request the same behavior with a prompt: "Don't tell me the answer. Give me one hint, and after I try, give me the next one."

## Study plans and mock exam analysis

Planning is an area where AI's error risk is low. Write your exam date, how many hours you can study each day, and your strong and weak topics, then ask for a weekly plan. Treat the plan as a draft: adjust the parts that don't fit your schedule, and at the end of each week note how much you completed and ask for an updated plan.

For mock exam analysis, enter the topic, correct, incorrect and blank counts into a table, then ask the AI to:

- Show which topics you are systematically losing points on
- Group your wrong answers into categories such as "knowledge gap," "careless error" and "time management problem"
- Recommend the three topics that will earn you the most points next week

Share only your own data; don't include personal information such as your national ID number. The resulting analysis is an interpretation, not a prediction of your actual exam score.

## Error risks in math and science questions

AI writes fluently, so a wrong solution can still look convincing. A [comparative study published in Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44217-026-01791-5) reported that ChatGPT-4o answered more of 10 questions correctly and made fewer math errors. However, the sample was small and the evaluation was done by hand. The result does not show that a specific tool is reliable on every question.

There is a similar warning in science. According to a March 2026 statement from Washington State University, [ChatGPT was accurate about 80% of the time in a test judging hundreds of scientific hypotheses as true or false](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/03/260317064452.htm). However, when the effect of guessing was accounted for, its performance dropped noticeably. The same statement notes that the model can give contradictory answers to the same question at different times.

In practice:

- Calculate the result yourself and compare it with the answer key in your book.
- Read every step of the solution. You can reach the right answer by a wrong method.
- If you doubt an answer, ask the question again and check whether the answers are consistent.
- Because of the tendency to fabricate information (hallucination), verify formulas and definitions against your textbook.

## The difference between copying and learning: what the research says

Getting a ready answer feels good in the moment. The real question is what remains once the AI is taken away. You can read about a randomized experiment involving roughly 1,000 students at a high school in Türkiye in a summary from Wharton. According to the study, [the group that used an unrestricted AI tool like ChatGPT performed 48% better than the control group during practice](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education/). When the AI was removed and an exam was given, the same group scored 17% lower. The experiment was conducted in the fall semester of 2023-2024.

The same study was [also published in PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422633122) and states that unrestricted AI use can seriously hinder learning. There is also a positive finding: students who used a version prepared with teacher input that gave hints rather than direct answers showed a largely reduced negative effect.

This finding comes from a math-focused experiment at a single school, so it should not be taken as a definitive result for every subject or student. Still, the direction is clear: scoring high during practice does not necessarily mean you have learned the material. The Ministry of National Education's [AI ethics recommendations](https://yegitek.meb.gov.tr/meb_iys_dosyalar/2025_10/22165423_yapay_zeka_etigi_tavsiyeleri_22102025_webformati.pdf) likewise advise that students understand AI outputs are not absolutely correct and avoid becoming dependent on AI alone.

To test yourself, set a simple rule: if you close the AI explanation and cannot solve a similar question on your own, you have not yet learned the topic. For personalized study methods, see our [personalized learning with AI](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ile-kisisellestirilmis-ogrenme/) guide.

## Following the rules in the exam hall

Using AI while studying at home and using it during an exam are completely different matters. According to the [2026 YKS application guide](https://dokuman.osym.gov.tr/pdfdokuman/2026/YKS/basvuru_kilavuz06022026.pdf) for the 2026 YKS (Turkey's university entrance exam), the identity details of candidates who cheat, attempt to cheat or give cheating assistance are recorded in a report, and a candidate who cheats can have their exam cancelled. Read the guidelines for each exam separately, as the specific rules may differ from one exam to another.

There is also a recent example. According to Haber Kenti, [a candidate in the 2026 YKS was arrested after photographing exam questions and uploading them to ChatGPT](https://www.haberkenti.com/yksde-yapay-zekali-kopya-girisimi-sorulari-chatgptye-yukleyen-aday-tutuklandi). The same report notes that entering the exam with mobile phones or similar devices is explicitly prohibited in the guidelines, and that photographing, copying and using exam questions is also banned.

So before the exam, review your devices and habits. Don't bring your phone, smartwatch or similar devices into the exam hall. If you are unsure of the rule, ask the proctors or check ÖSYM's current guidelines. For AI use at the school level, the rules set by your school and teacher apply.

## A short study routine

1. Have the AI explain the topic, then ask it to quiz you at the end.
2. Solve questions yourself first, and ask only for hints where you get stuck.
3. Verify the results and steps of your solutions with your textbook.
4. Have your weekly practice exam results analyzed and update your plan.
5. Solve similar questions with AI turned off to measure your actual level.

In this routine, AI saves you time, but in the exam, only what you have learned will carry you through. For more on developments in education, follow our [AI in education](/sektor/egitim/) section.
