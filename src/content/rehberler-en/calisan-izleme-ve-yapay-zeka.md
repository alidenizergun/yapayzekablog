AI-powered monitoring and productivity software promises to show what employees are doing and to score it. On the legal side, the question is: for what purpose, with how much data, and are you monitoring with the employee's knowledge?

This guide summarizes the limits set by KVKK (Turkey's Personal Data Protection Authority) and its Board's decisions in Turkey, and by the AI Act in the EU. It is for information only and is not legal advice. We reached most of the decisions through commentary in secondary sources, so consult a lawyer about your own company.

## Where the real risk lies in productivity tracking software

These tools typically collect data through email, communication channels, cameras or biometric systems; AI turns this data into scores or assessments. The Board decisions described here focus on specific channels: corporate email, biometric time tracking and cameras. We are not covering a separate decision for each type of software. However, the principles in these decisions, namely notice, purpose limitation and proportionality, are also a good yardstick when evaluating other monitoring tools. This inference is our own reading and not a binding interpretation.

If you are considering using monitoring data in performance reviews, you may also want to read the [AI in performance management](/rehber/performans-yonetiminde-yapay-zeka/) guide.

## Corporate email and communication channels: ruling 2026/2035

The Personal Data Protection Board adopted Principle Decision No. 2026/2035 on September 16, 2026. The decision was published in the Official Gazette on October 8, 2026, issue no. 33394. It covers the monitoring of communication channels used for work, above all corporate email accounts assigned by employers to employees. Employees must be informed in advance, and monitoring must be limited to its purpose and proportionate. Content review is only possible where there is concrete suspicion ([Alomaliye](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/10/08/isverenin-e-posta-denetimine-sinir/)).

The key points of the decision are:

- The Board ruled unlawful covert monitoring methods used without prior notice to employees, as well as audit tools that record all activity indiscriminately ([bianet](https://bianet.org/haber/kvkk-isveren-calisanin-e-postalarini-sinirsiz-denetleyemez-324218)).
- Access to content is only possible where there is concrete suspicion and the review is limited to a specific allegation.
- General and abstract notices stating only that corporate email accounts may be audited do not count as fulfilling the duty to inform ([Erikel Partners](https://www.erikelpartners.com.tr/yazi/kisisel-verileri-koruma-kurulunun-isyeri-iletisim-kanallarinin-denetimine-iliskin-ilke-karari-ve-huk)).
- Given the dependence and power imbalance in the employment relationship, the Board stated that explicit consent should not, as a rule, be the primary legal basis ([Kurşun & Partners](https://kursunpartners.com/tr/hukuk-bulteni/gelisme/kvkk-kurumsal-eposta-denetimi-ilke-karari-2026-2035/)).
- Data obtained from an audit can only be accessed by a limited number of staff assigned to that task ([Baba Ocağı](https://www.babaocagi.com.tr/kvkk-acikladi-isverenler-calisanlarin-yazismalarini-kafasina-gore-inceleyemeyecek)).

In short, justifying a system that continuously scans and scores all correspondence appears difficult within this framework.

## Biometric time tracking: ruling 2026/921

The Board's decision No. 2026/921, dated April 29, 2026, was published in the Official Gazette on June 2, 2026. The decision covers methods such as fingerprints, facial recognition, and iris and retina scans. The Board classified biometric data as special category personal data ([Alomaliye](https://www.alomaliye.com/2026/06/02/mesai-takibinde-biyometrik-veri-islenmesine-yeni-ilke-karari-kvkk/)).

According to KVKK's official announcement, biometric processing carried out solely for time tracking does not rely on any of the processing conditions in KVKK Article 6. Even where valid explicit consent exists, the processing does not meet the proportionality criterion in Article 4 ([KVKK](https://www.kvkk.gov.tr/Icerik/8912/29-04-2026-tarihli-ve-2026-921-sayili-mesai-takibi-amaciyla-biyometrik-veri-islenmesi-hakkinda-ilke-karari-na-iliskin-gorus-talepleri-hakkinda-kamuoyu-duyurusu)). Practical takeaway: if you can achieve the same purpose with less data, for example with card-based access, biometrics should not be used.

Some online commentary describes this decision as also covering camera and location tracking. The official announcement addresses only biometric time tracking, so we do not make any definitive claims about cameras or location based on this decision. We also have not included the fine amounts reported in some sources, because we could not verify them in the official text.

## Cameras: June 8, 2026 announcement

On June 8, 2026, KVKK published a public announcement on security cameras in workplaces. The announcement stresses that monitoring employees for performance and efficiency purposes will not be considered legitimate. Cameras are not banned outright: they may be used for purposes such as occupational health and safety, preventing workplace accidents, workplace security, and the prevention and detection of crime ([KP Legal](https://kplegal.com.tr/is-yerlerinde-guvenlik-kamerasi-kullanimina-iliskin-kvkkdan-onemli-kamuoyu-duyurusu-yayimlandi/)).

Cameras should not be placed in private areas such as toilets, changing rooms, prayer rooms and rest areas ([Universal Hukuk](https://www.universalhukuk.com/is-yerlerinde-guvenlik-kamerasi-kamuoyu-duyurusu)). Using camera footage with an AI analytics layer to measure workers' movement carries a risk of conflicting with this distinction in the announcement.

## The EU's ban on emotion recognition in the workplace

Article 5(1)(f) of the EU's AI Act prohibits AI systems from inferring people's emotions in workplaces and educational institutions. The ban has applied since February 2025 ([regulation-ai.eu](https://www.regulation-ai.eu/en/prohibited-practices/)). The scope is narrow: the ban applies to systems that infer emotions from biometric data, while systems that infer emotions from written text may fall outside it ([Wolters Kluwer](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/global-workplace-law-and-policy/the-prohibition-of-ai-emotion-recognition-technologies-in-the-workplace-under-the-ai-act/)).

There are two exceptions: medical and safety purposes. As a safety example, detecting drowsiness in a vehicle driver is cited. Detecting a customer's anger in a call center is also not considered covered by the ban. However, if the system simultaneously analyzes the representative's emotions, it requires separate assessment ([regulation-ai.eu](https://www.regulation-ai.eu/en/prohibited-practices/)).

The ban is an EU regulation and has no direct equivalent in Turkey. If you have ties to the EU, see the [EU AI Act and Turkey](/rehber/ab-yapay-zeka-yasasi-ve-turkiye/) guide.

## Transparency and proportionality: a practical checklist

The common thread across the decisions is two principles: employees should know what is being monitored and why (transparency), and monitoring should not go beyond what the purpose requires (proportionality). Before buying a monitoring tool, ask:

1. **Is the purpose written down?** Define the purpose from the start, such as security or occupational health, and do not later expand it to performance monitoring. The camera announcement also requires this ([ODS Avukatlık](https://www.odsavukatlik.com/news-insights/ris-yerlerinde-kvkk-uygulamalarina-dair-guncel-gelismeler/)).
2. **Is there a less intrusive way?** If the same result can be achieved with less data, a proportionality problem arises.
3. **Is the notice specific?** A general sentence is not enough. State clearly which channel is monitored, for what purpose, and how it is audited.
4. **Is access restricted?** Only a small number of assigned people should be able to access the data.
5. **Is the retention period reasonable?** Keeping records longer than necessary is a point of criticism.
6. **Did you ask the vendor?** Find out whether the tool has functions such as emotion inference, biometric data processing or recording all correspondence.

For HR teams, the broader framework is on the [AI in human resources](/rehber/insan-kaynaklarinda-yapay-zeka/) page.

## Limits and consulting an expert

We reviewed most of the decisions in this area through secondary sources; we could not verify the text of ruling 2026/2035 on the official site. It should also be remembered that principle decisions do not directly bind the courts. Liability may not be limited to administrative action alone: according to a law firm's summary, the path to compensation is also open. Therefore, before deploying a new monitoring system, consult the expert responsible for personal data compliance in your organization or a lawyer, and check the details against the official text.
