Using a virtual influencer or AI avatar appears attractive for brands: the character is always available, consistent and under the brand's control. However, advertising rules in Turkey are changing quickly in this area, and the margin for error is small. This guide summarizes what virtual characters can do, what the Advertising Board (Reklam Kurulu) guidelines and the new regulation change expect from brands, and how to start a trial safely. It is not legal advice; you should consult an expert before launching a campaign.

## What is a virtual influencer or AI avatar?

The two concepts are often confused. A virtual influencer is a fully fictional character with its own account, personality and content stream, which works with brands. An AI avatar is usually a digital presenter created with generative AI that speaks in a video or presentation. A third case is a digital copy of a real person created with AI. This third group is the most legally sensitive, and as you will see below, the regulation draws its boundary precisely here.

This distinction has practical consequences: the law imposes different obligations depending on whether the character is fictional, mimics a real person, or appears indistinguishable from a human. Before starting a campaign plan, determine which category you fall into.

## Opportunities for brands

The benefits commonly cited for virtual characters include full control over appearance and tone, a consistent identity across different campaigns, and flexibility in shooting and production. With avatar videos, it is also relatively easier to adapt the same script into different languages or formats.

All of these depend on the brand's own goals. This guide does not include any performance or cost figures that we have verified, so general promises such as "cheaper" or "more effective" should be avoided. Running a small pilot and measuring the results with your own data is the most reliable approach. ## The core framework in Turkey: Advertising Board guidelines

Although virtual characters are new, the rules were originally written for traditional influencer marketing. The Advertising Board's 2021 guidelines require that advertising in social media influencers' commercial posts be open, clear and distinguishable, and [prohibit](https://moral.av.tr/tr/hukuki-haberler/sosyal-medya-etkileyicileri-influencer-tarafindan-yapilan-ticari-reklam-ve-uygulamalar-hakkinda-kilavuz-yayimlandi-508) covert advertising in audio, text or visual form.

Posts that involve financial compensation or free products must also clearly disclose this. Among the sample terms in the guidelines are #Reklam, #Sponsor and #İş birliği ([partnership](https://gun.av.tr/media/vuml04fo/sosyal-medya-etkileyicileri-tarafindan-yapilan-ticari-reklam-ve-haksiz-ticari-uygulamalar-hakkinda-kilavuz-yayimlandi.pdf)). One legal commentary does not consider the term "partnership" alone sufficient and expects an advertising or promotion term. Considering the difference between these two views, the safest approach is to explicitly use the word "ad" in the label.

Another important point is responsibility. The guidelines assign obligations separately to three parties: the advertiser, the advertising agency and the media organization. An advertiser cannot [escape](https://yildirimhukuk.av.tr/hukuki-bilgilendirmeler/208/reklam-kurulu-tarafindan-sosyal-medya-araciligi-ile-yapilan-reklamlara-iliskin-y) its obligations by claiming that "the person who posted is responsible for their own content." If you manage a virtual character or have an agency manage it for you, this responsibility remains with you.

## New regulation change for AI characters

The change to the advertising regulation was published in the Official Gazette (Resmî Gazete) dated July 1, 2026, numbered 33297, and the provisions will [take effect](https://www.hukukihaber.net/yapay-zeka-ile-uretilmistir-etiketi-her-reklami-hukuka-uygun-hale-getirir-mi) on August 1, 2026. Two points stand out in terms of content:

- If an ad uses a digital character created with AI that cannot be distinguished from a human, this must be disclosed clearly and in a way that is easy to recognize.
- Ads that give the impression that a digital copy of a real person, created with AI, has used or recommended a product are [banned](https://12punto.com.tr/gundem/ticaret-bakanligindan-reklam-yonetmeliginde-degisiklik-yapay-zeka-ile-olusturulan-reklamlara-duzenleme-143055).

The second provision is critical for avatar ads. Making a celebrity's or expert's image and voice imitate someone saying "I used this product" is not a problem that can be fixed with a label; it is directly prohibited. According to lawyer and legal writer Att. Fatma Tokat's assessment, in such content, the statement "produced with AI" does not [remove](https://www.hukukihaber.net/yapay-zeka-ile-uretilmistir-etiketi-her-reklami-hukuka-uygun-hale-getirir-mi) the ban. The same article says that Turkish companies remain liable even when they use a foreign AI service.

There are also differing interpretations. One article states that there is no uniform, general labeling rule for AI ads in Turkey, while another article from the same site, describing the August 1, 2026 regulation, says that ads created with AI [fall within](https://medyografya.com.tr/yapay-zek-reklami-nasil-etiketlenmeli) these rules. When the regulation change takes effect, it is necessary to check the text published in the Official Gazette. For the legal dimensions of imitation content, you can also review our guide to [deepfakes and the law](/rehber/deepfake-ve-hukuk/).

## Platform and EU rules

Platform rules on labeling AI content change frequently, and for this guide we do not have any verified details from official help pages. For this reason, we do not list individual platform rules. Before publishing, check the current advertising and AI content policy of every platform where you plan to run the campaign on its own help page.

On the EU side, some blogs state that the transparency rules of the EU AI Act (Article 50) began to apply on August 2, 2026, and that those publishing realistic AI content [must disclose](https://softtechniques.com/guides/ai-influencer-disclosure-rules) it. However, we could not access the official text for this date. Another source says that artistic, creative, humorous and fictional works may be exempt, but how this exemption applies to virtual influencer content is [unclear](https://keepingupwith.ai/articles/eu-ai-act-forces-virtual-influencer-industry-into-compliance-mode/). For campaigns targeting the EU market, it makes sense to get legal support. For the general framework, our guide to the [EU AI Act and Turkey](/rehber/ab-yapay-zeka-yasasi-ve-turkiye/) can be a starting point.

## Consumer trust and reputational risk

How virtual characters are received by consumers may vary depending on the brand, the audience and the product category. In this guide, we do not provide any consumer trust figures that we can verify with sources; do not read industry surveys as definitive results without comparing them with your own audience.

Several concrete principles can help manage reputational risk:

- Do not hide the fact that the character is artificial; concealing it creates both legal and reputational risk.
- Avoid claims that require real experience ("I used it, and it worked"). A virtual character cannot experience a product.
- Do not inflate a character's visibility with fake reviews or artificial engagement; our guide to [fake reviews and AI](/rehber/sahte-yorum-ve-yapay-zeka/) is useful on this topic.

## How to start: a short checklist

1. **Define the character type.** Is it a fictional character, a digital copy of a real person, or an appearance indistinguishable from a human?
2. **Stay away from real-person copies.** If a person's image or voice will be used, obtain written permission and do not give the impression that they experienced or recommended the product.
3. **Make the disclosure visible.** Provide the ad and AI information in a noticeable place, in clear language; if necessary, also include the word "ad."
4. **Write responsibility into the contract.** Since the advertiser, agency and media organization share the same responsibility, define the content approval process clearly in the contract.
5. **Start with a pilot.** Test on a single platform with a short campaign; measure audience reaction and possible complaints.
6. **Set internal rules.** Document which AI tools your team may use and with what data; our guide to [creating social media content with AI](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ile-sosyal-medya-icerigi-uretme/) offers ideas for workflows.

## Limits and uncertainties

A Turkish-language search found no virtual influencer campaign in Turkey and no Advertising Board decision on this topic, so we do not yet know how sanctions will be applied in practice. A legal blog contains figures on penalty amounts, but since they appear in only one source, we do not report them here. The regulation change takes effect on August 1, 2026, and interpretations will become clearer after that date. Until then, be cautious: clearly disclose the character, avoid imitating real people, and get expert advice before the campaign.
