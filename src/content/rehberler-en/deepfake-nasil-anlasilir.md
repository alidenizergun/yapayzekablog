A deepfake is fake content generated by AI that shows a real person doing something they did not do or saying something they did not say. Deepfakes show up in many areas, from fake investment ads featuring celebrities to fabricated political statements to scam calls that imitate the voices of loved ones. Because the technology is improving quickly, it is better to use several methods together rather than rely on a single clue.

## 1. Look at the context first

Question how the content reached you: Was it published by a reputable news outlet, or shared by an account you don't know? An ad in which a famous person promises "guaranteed returns," a shocking statement or a message demanding urgent money are the most typical examples of deepfake scams.

## 2. Look for visual inconsistencies in video

- Blurring or flickering at the edges of the face, the hairline and the neck
- Unnatural blinking, a fixed stare or odd facial expressions
- Lip movements that don't quite match the audio
- Distortions in teeth, ears, hands and fingers
- Light and shadow on the face that don't match the environment
- Accessories such as glasses or earrings that appear and disappear

## 3. What to listen for in audio

- Monotone, emotionless or robotic intonation
- Breathing sounds that are missing or that occur in odd places
- Word choices that don't fit the person's known way of speaking
- Background noise that cuts off abruptly or sounds artificial

## 4. What to check in photos

- Text in the background that is meaningless or garbled
- Oddities in the number of hands and fingers
- Overly smooth, plastic-looking skin
- Asymmetrical earrings, glasses or clothing details
- Shadows falling in different directions

## 5. Verify the source

Visual checks are increasingly insufficient, so verification is the most reliable method:

- **Reverse image search:** Run the image, or a frame from the video, through image search engines to try to find the original source.
- **Official accounts:** Check whether the same statement appears on the person's or organization's official website and verified accounts.
- **Fact-checking platforms:** Check the websites of independent fact-checking organizations in Turkey and around the world.
- **Multiple sources:** A real event usually appears in many reliable sources.

## 6. Check for content labels

Some platforms and AI companies add labels or digital watermarks to AI-generated content. The presence of such labels helps, but their absence does not show that the content is real.

## 7. Stop before you share

Don't share content you suspect is fake; sharing helps spread disinformation. Report content you believe is fake using the platform's reporting feature. When you receive urgent requests for money or information, verify by calling the other party on another channel you know.
