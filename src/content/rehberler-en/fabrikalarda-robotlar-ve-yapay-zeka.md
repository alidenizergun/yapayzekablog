Robots in factories are not new. What is new is that AI is changing the conversation around both traditional industrial robots and humanoid robots. In this guide, we bring together the latest data from the International Federation of Robotics (IFR), Türkiye's position, and the humanoid robot factory trials that can be verified with sources. The aim is to help you separate what each figure says from what it does not say.

## Global picture: 5 million robots in factories

According to the IFR's [2026 report](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/five-million-robots-now-operate-in-factories-globally), the active stock of industrial robots in factories worldwide reached 5 million by the end of 2025, a 9% increase over the previous year. The same report says more than 600,000 new robots were installed in factories in 2025, an 11% increase. Some summary sites circulate more precise figures, but the IFR's own page only uses rounded numbers, so we stick with those.

The IFR also shares a forecast: annual new installations are expected to rise to 655,000 in 2026 and 806,000 in 2029. Keep in mind that this is a projection, not a confirmed outcome.

## Where installations are concentrated

[According to The Robot Report](https://www.therobotreport.com/5-million-robots-now-working-factories-worldwide-ifr-reports/), China installed 354,000 new robots in 2025. The US overtook Japan with 38,400 installations to become the second-largest market. Installations in Japan fell 19% to 36,200. In other words, global growth is not a story that advances at the same pace in every country: China's share is decisive, while some major markets are slowing.

Most of these robots are still traditional industrial robots: arms that perform pre-programmed, repetitive tasks at high speed from a fixed point. AI is adding capabilities such as vision, error detection and flexible programming to these arms. For details, see our guide to [robots and AI in factories](/rehber/fabrikalarda-robotlar-ve-yapay-zeka/).

## Robot density and installations in Türkiye

The picture for Türkiye is mixed. According to [IFR data reported by N24](https://n24.com.tr/teknoloji/ifr-turkiyede-robot-stoku-31-bine-cikti-dunya-5-milyonu-asti), the active robot stock rose 7% to 30,990 units, its highest level ever. On the other hand, [according to figures reported by Bursa Manşet](https://www.bursamanset.com/turkiye-de-fabrikalardaki-robot-sayisi-rekor-kirdi/140350/), new installations fell 9% in 2025 to 3,194 units. This followed a record of 4,429 in 2023, and marks two consecutive years of decline. The stock is growing, but the pace of growth is slowing.

In manufacturing, Türkiye has 52 robots per 10,000 employees; the figure is 374 in automotive and 35 in other sectors. The density in Germany, Japan, South Korea and the US is far above Türkiye's. Be careful when comparing: Türkiye's data is from 2025, while the data for other countries is from the IFR's 2024 comparative dataset. For this reason, read the comparison as a general ranking rather than a direct side-by-side match. The same report, as covered in the press, notes that despite Türkiye's young and educated population, a skills gap in technical positions persists. For an overview of the Turkish landscape, the guide to [AI in Turkish industry](/rehber/turkiyede-sanayide-yapay-zeka/) is also useful.

## Humanoid robots: Figure and the BMW trial

One of the best-documented examples of humanoid robots, based on sources, is Figure's work at BMW's Spartanburg plant. [According to Figure's own announcement](https://www.figure.ai/news/production-at-bmw), Figure 02 robots worked at the factory for 11 months, loaded more than 90,000 parts, completed more than 1,250 working hours and contributed to the production of more than 30,000 X3 vehicles. The robots worked 10-hour shifts, Monday through Friday.

The company also shares its targets: an 84-second cycle time (37 seconds of which is loading), placement accuracy above 99% per shift, and zero interventions. These are targets the company defined itself, so do not read them as achieved results. There is also no independent verification of any of these figures. BMW has not disclosed the number of robots, the contract value or the duration.

An interesting detail concerns durability. [According to IIoT World](https://www.iiot-world.com/artificial-intelligence-ml/robotics/physical-ai-deployment-roi-humanoid-robots/), Figure says most failures occurred in the arm section, and that 11 months of data led to the development of a new generation of robots. On duration, some sources say 10 months; we based our figure on the 11-month statement on Figure's own page.

## A humanoid robot start-up in Türkiye: DOF Robotics

There is also a concrete investment from Türkiye, but it is at a different stage. [According to Istiklal's report](https://www.istiklal.com.tr/teknoloji/25-milyon-dolarlik-yatirim-turkiyede-insansi-robot-fabrikasi-kuruluyor-1096638h), DOF Robotics is setting up a facility called DOF TECH in the Kuzey Marmara Special Industrial Zone in Arnavutköy, Istanbul. The company describes it as Türkiye's first integrated service and humanoid robot manufacturing facility. The investment budget is $25 million, and the announcement was made on June 17, 2026. The company says the investment focuses on service robots, AI and humanoid robots.

[According to Teknotalk](https://www.teknotalk.com/dof-robotics-turk-muhendisligiyle-insansi-robot-uretimine-hazirlaniyor-206249/), the company plans to first start humanoid robot development and prototype production at this facility, and then move to serial production as capacity increases. No date has been given for serial production. We could not find in the company's statement the military robot claims circulating in the media. There is also no source showing that this facility is running a humanoid robot on a production line; the project is in the planning and prototype stage. For this reason, evaluate DOF Robotics today not as a production solution, but as a domestic manufacturing start-up.

## Realistic expectations and limits

The following conclusions can be drawn from this data:

- **Traditional robots are mature and widespread.** A stock of 5 million shows that this technology has been part of manufacturing for years. Quick returns are mostly seen in well-defined tasks within this area.
- **Humanoid robots are at an early stage.** The most concrete example we have is at a single factory, on a narrow task (parts loading), and is documented by the company's own statements. Reading it as general proof of scale would be misleading.
- **Durability is an open question.** Figure itself acknowledging arm failures shows that hardware reliability is still one of the main limits.
- **In Türkiye, demand and installations are not moving in the same direction.** While the stock hits a record, new installations have declined for two years. The sources do not explain why, so we do not offer an interpretation.

## How a business can get started

If you are a business considering robot investment, rather than waiting for humanoid robots, first identify which tasks are repetitive, well-defined and measurable. Vision-based applications such as quality control can be a lower-risk starting point; see our guide to [image processing for quality control](/rehber/goruntu-isleme-ile-kalite-kontrol/). Run a pilot project on a single line with clear metrics (cycle time, error rate, number of interventions), and test vendor claims against your own data. For the broader framework, see the guide to [AI in manufacturing](/rehber/uretimde-yapay-zeka/) and the [AI in industry](/sektor/sanayi/) section.

Finally, do not leave occupational safety and worker involvement out of the plan. For teams sharing a space with robots, training and safety rules are as important as technical specifications.
