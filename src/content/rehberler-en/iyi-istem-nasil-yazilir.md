The quality of the answers you get from AI assistants depends largely on what you ask them. Told to "summarize this," the same model produces an ordinary summary; when you explain clearly what you want, it produces text you can use directly. The seven steps below work with ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and all similar chat assistants.

## 1. State the task clearly in a single sentence

The first sentence of your prompt should tell the model exactly what to do. Instead of "Write something about marketing," a task definition such as "Write a three-sentence Instagram promo for a newly opened coffee shop" leaves the model no need to guess.

## 2. Provide the context

The model does not know your company, your customer or your project. The more relevant information you give, the more accurate the answer: who the audience is, where the text will be used, what has been tried before, and what matters. If you are working from a long document, paste the document in directly; this works better than describing a summary of it.

## 3. Specify the audience and tone

A phrase such as "for executives," "for customers with no technical background" or "in a way a 10-year-old would understand" directly changes the model's word choice and level of detail. State the tone as well: formal, friendly, persuasive, neutral.

## 4. Describe the format you want

Say what the output should look like: a bulleted list, a table, an email, a 150-word paragraph? Add details such as length, use of headings and language. For example: "Give the result as a 5-item list; each item should be no more than two sentences."

## 5. Show an example

Including a sample of text you like is more effective than long explanations. You can share a paragraph and say "Write in this style:" or provide an example of the output format you want. The model imitates the structure and tone of the example.

## 6. Write down constraints and prohibitions

Say what you don't want, too: "Don't make up numbers; flag information you're unsure about," "Don't use technical terms," "Don't mention competing brands." Especially in fact-based work, asking the model to cite sources or flag anything it is unsure about reduces the risk of fabricated information (hallucination).

## 7. Check the answer and refine the prompt

If the first answer isn't what you expected, rather than starting over, say what is missing: "Make it shorter," "Expand the second item with a concrete example," "Make the tone more formal." For a task you perform often, you can save a prompt that works well and reuse it as a template.

## Example: a weak prompt and a strong prompt

**Weak prompt:** "Summarize my meeting notes."

**Strong prompt:** "Summarize the following project meeting notes for executives who did not attend the meeting. First write a two-sentence overview, then list the decisions made and who is responsible for each, as bullet points. Keep dates and figures exactly as they are; do not add any information that is not in the notes."

Because the second prompt provides the task, audience, format and constraints together, it produces a usable result on the first try.

## Things to watch out for

- Always verify the numbers, dates, names and quotations produced by AI in important work.
- Before entering personal data and company secrets into prompts, find out the data policy of the tool you are using.
- When working with long documents, remember that you can exceed the model's context window limit; provide very long content in sections.
