Creating product photos and videos with AI looks like a cheap way to prepare visuals without setting up a studio for a small store. It does have real uses: cleaning up backgrounds, showing a product in a different setting, and producing a short promotional video. But the same tools also carry the risk of making a product look better, larger or a different color than it really is. This guide explains where to start and where to stop. Legal topics are general information and do not replace legal advice.

## Background replacement: the lowest-risk starting point

Testing with background replacement first makes sense, because you are not altering the product itself. You take a properly shot photo and simplify the background or add a different setting. The general approach:

- Shoot a sharp, well-lit source photo that shows the whole product. A phone camera can be enough to start; a poor source photo will not be fixed even by a good tool.
- Remove the background and use a simple backdrop, or describe a setting that suits the product. When writing the prompt, describe the setting, lighting and angle concretely. For more detail, see the [guide to writing good prompts](/rehber/iyi-istem-nasil-yazilir/).
- Check the edges closely. Cutting errors are common in fine details such as hair strands, transparent surfaces and shoelaces.
- Check shadows and reflections. If the product looks pasted onto the setting, customers will notice.

For a more detailed introduction to how these tools work, see the [guide to AI product photography](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ile-urun-fotografi/). If you want a seller-focused overview, the [AI page for marketplace sellers](/rehber/yapay-zeka-ile-urun-fotografi/) may also help.

## Model images: dressing and showing the product

For clothing, accessories and similar products, it is possible to use AI to generate images of a model wearing the item. There are two separate problems here. The first is preserving the product's true appearance: fabric texture, print placement, cut, color tone and size proportions. Generative models redraw the product and may change details; they can add elements that do not exist, such as text or buttons, or remove ones that do. The second is the source of the human image: faces that resemble a real person raise questions of consent and personality rights.

In practice:

- Place the output next to the real product and compare them. Check color, pattern, buttons, pockets and stitching one by one.
- Even if the generated model does not resemble a real person, check whether the tool's terms allow commercial use.
- Do not let the body or posture be misleading. An image that shows how the product fits differently from reality will lead to customer returns.

You can learn how AI-generated human images are identified and why they should be used carefully in the [guide on how deepfakes are recognized](/rehber/deepfake-nasil-anlasilir/).

## Product video: keep it short and honest

AI product video is used to add motion to a still photo or to create a short promotional clip. There are Turkish-language articles on this for Trendyol specifically; for example, [Veritas Dijital's guide](https://www.veritasdijital.com/trendyol-yapay-zeka-ile-urun-videosu-nasil-olusturulur/) covers how to prepare an AI product video. However, most such articles come from agencies and tool vendors, so confirm the rules on the marketplace's own pages.

What to watch for in video:

- Motion should reflect the product's real function. Showing it doing something it cannot do is misleading.
- Keep it short and show the product clearly. Flashy transitions and constantly changing backgrounds can make the product hard to understand.
- If you use a voiceover or synthetic presenter, do not hide that it is artificial.
- Watch every frame: in AI videos, fingers, text and small objects can distort from frame to frame.

## What to look for when choosing a tool

There are many tools, so choose based on your needs rather than trying them all. For comparison lists, see [the best tools by use case](/en-iyi/). For a Turkish-language summary of tools, you can read [Ideasoft's article](https://www.ideasoft.com.tr/yapay-zeka-e-ticaret-fotografi-yapma/). When choosing, ask these questions:

- How faithfully does it preserve product details? Test three to five products on the same tool with a small sample set.
- Is commercial use of the output permitted?
- Is it clear how uploaded product photos are stored and whether they are used for training?
- Does the output resolution match the image size the marketplace requires?

If your uploaded photos show people, addresses or documents, also review the [guide on protecting personal data](/rehber/yapay-zeka-kullanirken-kisisel-verileri-koruma/).

## Showing a product differently from how it looks: consumer law

This is the biggest risk. A product image directly shapes a consumer's purchase decision. An image in which AI has brightened the color, erased flaws, exaggerated the size or added a nonexistent accessory can mislead the consumer. A [complaint thread on Şikayetvar](https://www.sikayetvar.com/trendyol/trendyolda-yapay-zeka-ile-hazirlanan-urun-gorselleri-tuketiciyi-yaniltiyor), a Turkish consumer complaint platform, shows that consumers can find AI-prepared product images misleading. Such complaints can lead to returns, negative reviews and reputational damage.

In Turkey, the framework for advertising and commercial practices is set out in the [Regulation on Commercial Advertising and Unfair Commercial Practices](https://www.mevzuat.gov.tr/anasayfa/MevzuatFihristDetayIframe?MevzuatNo=20435&MevzuatTertip=5&MevzuatTur=7). Read the current text of the regulation. For how AI-generated images should be assessed case by case, get a lawyer's opinion. The general principle is simple: the image must represent the product's real state.

For a safe approach, use this distinction: changing the background or setting generally does not change the product; changing its color, texture, size, function or content does. Avoid the latter.

## Marketplace rules: check before uploading

Marketplaces frequently update their image standards, and their approaches to AI-generated images can differ. Sellers often ask, 'Is AI product photography allowed on Trendyol?' On this, there are blog posts such as [Digital Kafalar's article](https://www.digitalkafalar.com/trendyolda-yapay-zeka-urun-fotografi-kullanilir/). Do not assume the rules described in blogs are current: the seller panel, help pages and announcements are what count.

Before uploading, check:

- The background, size and aspect ratio requirements for the marketplace's main image.
- Whether AI-generated images must be separately disclosed or labeled.
- The penalties for product images that do not match the product, such as image removal or an account warning.

If you sell on more than one marketplace, read each one's rules separately; an image that causes no problems on one may cause them on another.

## Copyright and model rights

Who owns the copyright to an AI-generated image is debated in Turkish legal literature. Sources such as [Bilişim ve Hukuk](https://bilisimvehukuk.net/icerik/yapay-zeka-ile-uretilen-gorsellerin-telif-hakki-kime-aittir) address this question, but do not expect a single definitive answer. The practical takeaway: for images that will form your brand's lasting identity, such as logos, packaging and campaign visuals, clarify the legal position with a lawyer in advance.

Do not imitate someone else's work or a well-known style when generating images. If your product photo includes another brand's logo, a famous character or a real person's face, these can create separate rights issues. For legal assessments of AI processing of real people's images, you can consult sources such as [attorney Buğra Topaktaş's article](https://topaktas.av.tr/yapay-zeka-deepfake-hukuki-degerlendirme/). If you show your product on a person, make sure you have that person's written consent and that the scope of the consent, including duration and media, is clear.

## Checklist before publishing

1. I placed the image next to the real product; color, size and details match.
2. No part, feature or accessory that does not exist has been added to the product.
3. I have read the marketplace's current image rules on its own page.
4. I checked the commercial use terms of the tool I used.
5. If there is a face that resembles a real person, permission and rights status are clear.
6. There is no other brand's logo, character or well-known design.
7. If a customer complaint comes in, I have the source photo and production record on hand.

Start small: test background replacement on a few products, compare the results with the real product, and only then move on to model images and video. Speed should not come before accuracy.
