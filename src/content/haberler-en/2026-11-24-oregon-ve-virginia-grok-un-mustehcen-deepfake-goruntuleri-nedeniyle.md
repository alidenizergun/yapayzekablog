Oregon Attorney General Dan Rayfield and Virginia Attorney General Jay Jones filed two separate but similar lawsuits on Monday. According to The Verge, the suits allege that SpaceX violated state consumer protection laws. The complaints claim the company ignored industry-standard safety measures and misrepresented the safety of the X platform and its Grok AI features.

The complaint filed in Virginia argues that Elon Musk knowingly ignored growing concerns about AI-generated sexual deepfakes and saw a business opportunity in them. The petition describes Grok's weak safeguards as "not a bug, but a feature." In a press statement, Jones said X and xAI told users they were protected, and that this was not true. According to Jones, the tool allowed nonconsensual intimate images and child sexual abuse material to be created and spread.

The complaints focus mainly on Grok Imagine, the tool that lets Grok and X users generate images and videos. xAI, the company Musk later merged with SpaceX, introduced a "spicy" mode for Grok Imagine in August 2025. In January 2026, X users were able to edit any image without the permission of the person who posted it. According to The Verge, X was then flooded with nonconsensual sexual deepfakes of adults and children. xAI made some features paid and blocked certain requests, but tests by The Verge found that these restrictions did little to stop deepfake generation.

Both lawsuits seek monetary damages. In a statement issued after the suits were announced, the X Safety team said people who try to generate such content knowingly violate the terms of service and work to circumvent safeguards. The team said it has tightened protections designed to refuse sexual images of minors or of real people, and uses classifiers that scan every prompt before an image is generated and every output afterward.
