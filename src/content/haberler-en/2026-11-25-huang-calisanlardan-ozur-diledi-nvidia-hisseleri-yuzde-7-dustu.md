Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang apologized to employees for violating the company's code of conduct in his first all-hands meeting since returning to the role. According to the remarks reviewed by Wired, Huang said he had made "very serious personal mistakes" and had therefore resigned. Saying he had hurt his family and his wife, Huang added that he goes to therapy for an hour and a half every day, and that it helps him a great deal.

Huang said he wrote most of the code of conduct himself and that violations would not be tolerated, but that every violation does not automatically mean dismissal. He also rejected rumors that he had returned because he was a "tech brother." He acknowledged that his harsh management style had at times worn employees down. He said he would undergo a 360-degree performance review, collect feedback at most once a month, and did not plan to roll out this practice company-wide.

## Shares fall

According to CNBC, Nvidia shares fell about 7% on Tuesday. The stock had closed more than 30% higher on Monday after Huang's return. Midday, Micron lost 10%, AMD about 9% and Marvell 8%. Arm fell more than 7%. Bank of America warned that the reappointment did not mean the end of reviews.

## Attorney general and FBI director stay silent

Trump told CNBC that he had given assurances to the board and that he had "made the deal." Attorney General Todd Blanche and FBI Director Kash Patel, however, left reporters' questions about the investigation into Huang unanswered on Tuesday. Blanche only said that Trump had spoken with many people, adding that he would not engage in correspondence with the president. Patel did not answer whether the FBI had met with Huang.

According to Nvidia's statement on Monday, the board believes, based on a view received from senior levels of the US government, that the reappointment does not pose a national security risk. According to Wired, the board is continuing its investigation into whether Huang was a target of foreign intelligence.
