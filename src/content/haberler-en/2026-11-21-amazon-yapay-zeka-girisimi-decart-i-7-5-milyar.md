The Financial Times (FT), citing sources close to the matter, reported that Amazon is in advanced talks to acquire AI startup Decart. According to the report, Decart's value will be set at approximately $7.5 billion, and the deal could be announced as early as next week. No deal has been officially announced yet; the report is based on sources.

FT's sources say the talks have reached the final stage. The same sources warn that talks could still collapse. The Wall Street Journal had reported in October that Amazon was in talks to acquire Decart for approximately $7 billion.

## What does Decart do?

San Francisco-based Decart was founded in 2023 by Moshe Shalev and brothers Dean and Orian Leitersdorf, three Israeli engineers. According to the FT, the company's main software product helps developers switch between different AI chips. The company is also developing Lucy and Oasis world models, which can edit live video and generate interactive virtual worlds.

## AWS's competition with Nvidia

According to the FT, Decart was one of the early users of Trainium3 chips, which Amazon announced last year as a rival to Nvidia's powerful graphics processors. The report says that software that makes switching between chips easier is a significant competitive advantage at a time when the race to find additional computing power is accelerating.

The two companies' relationship is not new: a partnership between AWS and Decart was announced in December 2025. Under the agreement, Decart would optimize its Lucy model for real-time video generation on Trainium3 and offer its models through Amazon Bedrock. Decart, meanwhile, had received a $4 billion valuation in May after a $300 million round backed by investors including Sequoia Capital and Nvidia. The reported purchase price is well above that valuation.
