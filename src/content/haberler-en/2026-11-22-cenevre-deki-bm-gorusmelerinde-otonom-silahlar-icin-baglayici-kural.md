The Convention on Certain Conventional Weapons (CCW) conference in Geneva ended early Saturday without agreement on a mandate to negotiate binding rules for autonomous weapons, often called "killer robots." The outcome matched the expectation we reported in our November 16 story. Around 100 countries had called for negotiations on a binding ban; the United States, Israel, Belarus and the United Kingdom were among those opposing a dedicated negotiating mandate.

Countries agreed to begin discussing options. According to The Verge, two years were allotted for proposals on regulation, and some rules could remain "voluntary." Government experts will discuss additional elements until 2028 and present a proposal on whether the process should move toward a binding agreement. The date of the next meeting is not mentioned in the sources.

Russia blocked formal negotiations at the start of the meeting by refusing to approve the agenda. Because decisions were made by consensus, dpa described this stance as a de facto veto.
