U.S. President Donald Trump said at a White House press conference on Friday, November 20, that American companies will not be allowed to build data centers outside the country. According to The Verge, Trump announced that companies doing so would face a "tremendous tax" (tremendous tax). The press conference followed Trump's summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

According to Trump's remarks, companies could set up data centers abroad, but they would pay for that option through the tax. Citing China as the reason, Trump said that if the U.S. allowed it, China would "take all of them." He compared this to allowing chips to be manufactured overseas, arguing that the process had harmed the U.S.

## No concrete regulation yet

No legal regulation or formal draft stands behind the statement for now. Trump did not disclose the tax rate, which companies it would cover, or when it would take effect. According to IBTimes, he also did not explain what legal mechanism would be used to enforce the penalty. Trump said he had not yet told his own team about the plan and had announced it there; CNBC wrote that this was a surprise for his own team as well.

## State restrictions in the spotlight

The announcement came at a time when U.S. states and cities have placed restrictions on new data center applications. The Verge wrote that some states have imposed moratoriums and new limits, that companies may therefore have to look overseas, and that Trump's threat is aimed at making that option less attractive. In the same speech, Trump urged local governments not to shut down data centers, and instead to have companies pay their communities.

The Verge also noted that the new Congress, with a Democratic majority and set to take office in a few months, could make Trump's effort harder. The report also says some Republicans acknowledge that data center investments are not widely popular.
