According to a Wall Street Journal report, Jensen Huang, the Nvidia CEO who has resigned, developed close ties with Asian business partners that allowed Chinese companies to use advanced Nvidia chips outside China. The report says data centers in Southeast Asia exploited a loophole in US sanctions, laying the groundwork for alleged chip smuggling. Huang is also reported to have lobbied in Washington to keep this route open.

According to documents reviewed by the WSJ, Singapore-based Megaspeed procured roughly $12 billion worth of Nvidia chips for Chinese companies between March 2024 and June 2025. However, the records do not show how much of this hardware was actually delivered.

The report also notes that Malaysia is on track to become the world's second-largest data center hub after the US state of Virginia.

## Bloomberg's related report

According to a Bloomberg report published on November 13 and based on documents it reviewed, a Chinese company founded by Megaspeed executive Alice Huang illicitly obtained Nvidia chips and began using them in a data center in Shanghai. The report is also linked to Aperia, Nvidia's cloud partner in Singapore.

The information in both reports is based on documents reviewed by the outlets and has not been independently verified.
