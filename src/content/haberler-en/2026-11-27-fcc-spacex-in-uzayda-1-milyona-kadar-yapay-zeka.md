The U.S. Federal Communications Commission (FCC) on November 25 approved SpaceX's plan to deploy up to 1 million satellites for its Starmind orbital data center network. The decision, made by the FCC Space Bureau under delegated authority, is the first U.S. authorization for an orbital data center constellation, according to the agency. Starmind consists of satellites intended to run artificial intelligence computing workloads in space.

## Conditional and partial approval

The satellites will be placed in six orbital shells between 500 and 1,002 kilometers. SpaceX's original application had set the upper limit at 2,000 kilometers. Communications will mostly run over optical links with other satellites, including Starlink. The license also covers a backup Ka-band radio system for emergency data and command.

SpaceX's debris mitigation plan was found compliant only for satellites below 600 kilometers. Four of the six shells are above that line, and the company must file an amendment including a final debris mitigation plan before launching satellites into those orbits. A request to move some satellites into disposal orbits around Earth or the Sun, instead of reentering the atmosphere, was deferred due to a lack of information.

The timeline is also tight: the first Starmind satellite must be operational by November 25, 2033. After that, the target is 100,000 satellites by 2035, 500,000 by 2038 and 1 million by 2040. Missing any of these deadlines would invalidate the license.

## Statements and objections

FCC Chairman Brendan Carr said the plan opens new ground for the computing infrastructure needed for U.S. leadership in artificial intelligence. Elon Musk replied to Carr on X, writing that Starmind would be a major win for the United States and the world.

The decision rejected objections from Amazon's satellite unit Amazon Leo, Viasat and the American Astronomical Society (AAS). In its March filing, Amazon had described the plan as an aspirational goal rather than a real plan. The agency said the rules contain no provision limiting the number of satellites in a single constellation, and that astronomers' concerns about infrared brightness fall outside the scope of the radio license. AAS said it was disappointed by the decision.

## Nvidia partnership

In August, SpaceX announced that it would co-design the computing payload of the Starmind AI1 satellite with Nvidia. The satellites will feature Nvidia Rubin GPUs and Vera CPUs. According to Musk, AI1 could reach orbit as early as the third quarter of next year.
