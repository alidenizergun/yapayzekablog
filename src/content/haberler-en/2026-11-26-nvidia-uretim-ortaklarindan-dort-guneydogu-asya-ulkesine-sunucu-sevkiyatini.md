According to a Thursday Financial Times report citing three sources, Nvidia has asked manufacturing partners such as Dell and Supermicro to halt shipments of AI chip-equipped servers to Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Indonesia. The manufacturers have told their customers in those countries that Nvidia will not approve orders. According to the sources, Nvidia and its partners are trying to work out how to comply with Washington's new rules, and it is unclear whether the halt is temporary.

The move follows US rules announced last week that aim to stop Chinese companies from getting around restrictions by renting computing power from cloud providers using Nvidia chips. Cloud providers in the region have 90 days to apply for a license. One provider executive told the FT that the 90-day clock is running, but no one knows how to apply for a license. According to the FT, banks have halted roughly $500 million in loans for Singapore's Aolani tied to its planned deal with ByteDance.

The FT reported that Nvidia booked $104 billion in revenue through its Singapore affiliate in 2025, up 72% from the previous year. Nvidia also books part of its Taiwan and China revenue through this unit, so the figure does not reflect only sales in Singapore. Official responses from Nvidia and its partners were not included in the report.
