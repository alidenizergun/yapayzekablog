SpaceX had to pay a yield above 8% on the largest tranche of the $32 billion bond sale used to buy Nvidia chips. The report appeared in a November 20, 2026 article by Motley Fool.

To keep the debt off the company's balance sheet, the financing runs through a separate trust (an asset-management structure). The trust sells bonds, buys the chips and leases them to SpaceX. The chips are expected to start arriving in January.

According to the Financial Times, the lenders financing the deal want assurances that the financed chips will stay on Earth. It is unclear whether that assurance is binding.

SpaceX's AI unit, meanwhile, grew its revenue from $2.6 billion to $7.6 billion between the second and third quarters.
